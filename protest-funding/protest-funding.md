# Who pays for America's big protests? What the public record shows (2020–2026)

*Research file for swampforce.com. Compiled September 24, 2026 (MT). Nothing on the site was changed.*

**Sourcing rule used throughout:** a claim counts as **documented** only when a primary record supports it. Primary records here means:
- IRS Form 990 filings. ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer is used as a mirror, with a link to each filing.
- Foundations' own grant databases.
- FEC, USASpending and Lobbying Disclosure Act (LDA) filings.
- Court records and DOJ charging documents.
- Official government or company statements.
- Congressional records. These are labeled with the party whose majority or member wrote them.

Anything from news outlets, think tanks or social media is marked **reported, not confirmed by primary record**. Numbers are copied from filings; they are not estimates. Every row in `money-flows.csv` links to its filing.

## 1. Short answers in plain English

**Are protesters being paid?**
- Paid crowds are real. There is at least one company whose business is renting crowds, and courts and a city council have documented cases where it did so. Those documented cases were for a **corporation** (Entergy, in New Orleans) and for **a private individual in a business feud** (a South Carolina lawsuit).
- Paid or staged crowds have also been documented or charged on the **political right**: the 2015 Trump campaign announcement (reported) and 2016 pro-Trump rallies organized by Russian operatives (DOJ indictment).
- **We found no primary record** (court finding, prosecution, government report, IRS filing or company admission) showing that participants in the 2020 racial-justice protests, the 2024 campus/Gaza protests or the 2025–2026 "No Kings" protests were paid to attend.
- Several viral "paid protester" claims were checked and debunked. The June 2025 Los Angeles Craigslist ad was a prank. PolitiFact rated the 2026 Minnesota "paid agitators" claim False.

**Who funds the organizers?**
- The big national organizers are ordinary tax-exempt nonprofits with **paid staff and budgets**. That explains matching signs, tents, trainings and printed materials: it is evidence of **organization, not of paying attendees**.
- Their disclosed money comes mostly from large progressive foundations and donor-advised-fund sponsors: Open Society, Tides, Ford, Wallace Global, Heising-Simons, and the Arabella-managed funds.
- Examples from filings:
  - Indivisible, the main No Kings organizer, had $10.4M revenue in 2024.
  - Working Families Organization had $54.3M in 2024. Tides Foundation passed it $64.7M over 2019–2024.
  - The Movement for Black Lives received a $15M Open Society grant in 2021, through its fiscal sponsor.

**George Soros / Open Society?**
- **Yes**, Open Society entities have given documented, sizable grants to several protest-organizing groups. Per OSF's own database and its 990s:
  - Indivisible: $7.6M across 2017–2023.
  - MoveOn: $3.2M across 2020–2024 on the 990s.
  - Working Families: about $20M across 2020–2024 on the 990s.
  - Movement for Black Lives: $15M in 2021.
  - Sixteen Thirty Fund: $68M across 2020–2024.
- The stated purposes are general or advocacy support: "organizational support," "social welfare activities," "policy advocacy." **None is a grant to pay protesters.** OSF publicly says it does not pay people to protest.
- OSF's 2025 grants are not yet public, so **no primary record ties OSF money specifically to the 2025–2026 No Kings events**.
- Soros is also the largest *disclosed* individual political donor in this dataset: **$192.8M to federal committees in the 2020–2026 cycles** (FEC), including $175M to Democracy PAC II.

**Foreign adversaries?**
- **Documented:**
  - A U.S. intelligence statement (DNI, July 2024) said Iranian government actors posed as activists online, encouraged Gaza protests, and gave some financial support to protesters. It named no recipients and no amounts.
  - A 2018 DOJ indictment charged Russians with staging and paying for 2016 U.S. rallies, mostly pro-Trump.
- **Documented in filings, but the foreign-government link is alleged:** a network of U.S. nonprofits linked by reporting to American-born tech entrepreneur **Neville Roy Singham** (who lives in Shanghai) moved well over $100M. This includes $22.4M to The People's Forum, which helped organize Gaza protests.
  - The filings don't name Singham. His role comes from reporting and from a People's Forum statement quoted by Congress.
  - Republican-led committees allege Chinese Communist Party (CCP) ties and have issued subpoenas (2026). A grand jury probe was reported in June 2026.
  - **No charges, no FARA finding, and no court ruling** exists as of this writing.
- **No official finding** was located that Qatar or China funds U.S. protest groups. Qatar's money to U.S. **universities** is officially documented (over $1.1B in 2025, Dept. of Education), but that is money to schools, not protest groups.

**Taxpayer money?**
- **We found no federal grants** on USASpending to the main national protest organizers: Indivisible, MoveOn, Sunrise, People's Forum, CodePink, JVP, M4BL, Working Families, and the Open Society and Sixteen Thirty entities.
- Some groups that *took part* in protests do receive federal grants for specific programs. For example, CHIRLA in Los Angeles received $950K in DHS citizenship-education grants in FY2022–FY2024, plus $250K in 2014.
- Federal law already bars using grant money for lobbying or political activity.
- **No Inspector General or GAO finding** was located that grant funds paid for protests. Republican committee letters raising the question are requests, not findings.

**Dark money?**
- Yes, and **on both sides, at similar scale**. U.S. law lets most 501(c)(4) "social welfare" groups keep donors secret.
- The largest documented vehicles:
  - Left-aligned: **Sixteen Thirty Fund** ($389.7M revenue in 2020), **Future Forward USA Action** ($615.3M in 2024), **Majority Forward**.
  - Right-aligned: **Marble Freedom Trust** (Leonard Leo; received a reported $1.6B gift), **Concord Fund**, **One Nation**, **American Action Network**.
- Every attempt to pass the DISCLOSE Act failed in the Senate on party-line cloture votes (2010, 2012, 2022). Republicans voted no.

**Ties to politicians?**
- Many of these groups run affiliated PACs that support Democratic candidates, mostly through independent spending rather than direct checks.
- Senate Democratic Leader Schumer and other Democrats publicly urged people to attend No Kings. Speaker Johnson (R) called it a "Hate America rally."
- Leaders of Singham-linked and Gaza-protest groups gave small, legal FEC-reported contributions to some Democratic members.
- No federal earmarks to the main organizers were found.

## 3. Things the record does NOT support (said plainly)

1. **"Protesters at No Kings / BLM / Gaza marches are paid."** No court, prosecutor, agency or IRS filing we found shows payment to attendees of these protests. Groups do pay **staff and organizers**, and they pay for signs, stages, permits, sound systems and trainings, which is ordinary and legal. **Matching signs and tents show a funded organizing operation, not paid attendance.** Viral "proof" (the LA Craigslist ad, the Minnesota "$20/hour" video) was debunked.
2. **"Soros pays protesters."** OSF's grants are documented, large, and go to groups that organize protests. But the recorded purposes are general or advocacy support, and no primary record shows OSF paying attendees or funding No Kings events specifically. Whether you view the grants favorably or not, *funding an organization that holds protests* is different from *paying people to protest*, and the record shows only the first.
3. **"It's foreign adversaries."** The foreign role that is *documented* is limited: Iran's online influence and unspecified support (DNI), and Russia's 2016 operation (indictment). The Singham network is the largest foreign-*linked* money flow, but it runs through U.S. nonprofits funded by a U.S. citizen. The claim that it is directed by the Chinese government is an **uncharged allegation**. Nothing documented ties foreign money to Indivisible or No Kings.
4. **"It's taxpayer money."** No federal awards were found for the main national organizers. The one prominent case, CHIRLA, received citizenship-education grants, and no IG or GAO has found those funds were misused for protests.
5. **"Dark money is a left-wing problem" (or right-wing).** The filings show the largest vehicles on both sides are comparable in size.

## 4. Detail

### 4.1 Paid protesters and paid crowds

**What is documented**
- **Crowds on Demand** (crowdsondemand.com) markets hired crowds, protests and rallies nationwide on its own site.
- **Court record: *Bakala v. Krupa*, D.S.C. No. 9:18-cv-02590, Order of Judgment, 9/20/2021 (ECF 210).**
  - The court describes Crowds on Demand as an "astroturfing" company whose campaigns "are carried out using paid actors and operatives." It says Krupa engaged the firm to run a smear campaign that included "organizing protests using paid actors near Mr. Bakala's home."
  - Bakala settled with Swart and Crowds on Demand.
  - A default judgment of **$32,406,146.94** was entered against Krupa.
  - https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.scd.245575/gov.uscourts.scd.245575.210.0.pdf
- **New Orleans / Entergy (2017–2019).**
  - Entergy's own investigation found that a subcontractor paid people to attend and speak at City Council meetings on 10/16/2017 and 2/21/2018 in support of a gas plant. The subcontractor was Crowds on Demand, hired through the Hawthorn Group.
  - The Council imposed a **$5M penalty** on Entergy (Resolution R-19-78, 2019).
  - Sources: https://www.entergy.com/news/entergy-completes-internal-investigation-regarding-new-orleans-power-station-advocacy ; https://council.nola.gov/meetings/2019/20190314-regular-meeting/
- **Trump 2015 announcement.**
  - FEC filings show the Trump campaign (C00580100) paid **Gotham Government Relations & Communications $12,000 on 10/08/2015** for "EVENT CONSULTING" (image 201601319005280481).
  - That this covered paid extras at the June 2015 launch is **reported** (Hollywood Reporter), not stated in the filing.
- **Russia, 2016.** The DOJ indictment of the Internet Research Agency (2/16/2018) alleges the IRA paid Americans for rally stunts, including building a cage on a flatbed truck and hiring someone to pose as Hillary Clinton in it. These are **charges**; the case against the Concord entities was dismissed in 2020. The bipartisan Senate Intelligence report (Vol. 2) describes IRA-organized U.S. rallies.

**What is reported or alleged only**
- Crowds on Demand CEO Adam Swart said in July 2025 that he declined a roughly **$20M** offer to recruit demonstrators for "Good Trouble Lives On," and that he also turned down No Kings work. He named no client. *(NewsNation, Fox: reported, not confirmed.)*
- The June 2025 Los Angeles Craigslist "paid protester" ad was a prank *(FactCheck.org, AP, PolitiFact)*.
- PolitiFact rated the Jan. 2026 claim that Minnesota anti-ICE protesters were paid **False**.
- **No prosecutions** of anyone for paying No Kings, BLM or campus protesters were found.

### 4.6 Dark money: why donors can stay hidden

**Laws and court rulings**
- **26 U.S.C. 6104 / 6033:** exempt organizations must file 990s and make them public, but **contributor names are exempt from public disclosure**. The exceptions are private foundations and 527 political organizations.
- **IRS rule T.D. 9898 (2020):** most non-501(c)(3) groups, including 501(c)(4)s, no longer have to give the IRS donor names on Schedule B.
- **Treas. Reg. 1.501(c)(4)-1:** a 501(c)(4) must be "primarily" engaged in social welfare. It can do some political campaigning as long as that is not its primary activity. This lets c4s give to super PACs, and the super PAC reports the c4, not the c4's donors.
- **Supreme Court:**
  - ***NAACP v. Alabama* (1958):** forced disclosure of member lists can violate the First Amendment.
  - ***Citizens United v. FEC* (2010):** corporations, including nonprofits, may make unlimited independent expenditures.
  - ***AFP Foundation v. Bonta* (2021):** struck down California's blanket demand for charities' donor lists.
- **DISCLOSE Act votes**, all failed or blocked in the Senate:
  - House passed H.R.5175 on 6/24/2010, 219–206 (roll call 391; D 217–36, R 2–170).
  - Senate cloture failed:
    - 7/27/2010: 57–41.
    - 9/23/2010: 59–39.
    - 7/16/2012: 51–44.
    - 7/17/2012: 53–45.
    - 9/22/2022 on S.4822: 49–49, with all Democrats yes and all Republicans no.

### 4.8 Lobbying

**The law**
- **Lobbying Disclosure Act, 2 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.:** registration plus quarterly LD-2 reports, and semiannual LD-203 contribution reports.
- **First Amendment:** protects the right "to petition the Government for a redress of grievances."
- **18 U.S.C. 207:** cooling-off periods of 2 years for former senators and 1 year for former House members and senior staff.
- **Proposed reform:** the Close the Revolving Door Act of 2025 (H.R.3554 / S.1850) would impose a lifetime lobbying ban on former members and longer staff cooling-off periods. It was introduced 5/21/2025 and is pending in committee.

## 5. Methods and limitations

- **990 grants** were parsed from IRS e-file XML via ProPublica for the main funders (Schedule I and 990-PF Part XV), 2019–2025 filings. Where a funder amended a return, the latest filing per funder and period was kept.
  - Recipients were matched by name. **Name matching can err:** three look-alike organizations ("One Nation Life," "One Nation One Project," and a Knights of Columbus council) were caught and excluded.
  - Tides Center's 2020 filing lists $4.75M to "Black Lives Matter - Oklahoma City" with a mismatched EIN. It was **excluded** as likely anomalous.
- **Donor-advised funds** (Goldman Sachs Philanthropy Fund, Schwab Charitable, Fidelity Charitable, National Philanthropic Trust, Tides Foundation, DonorsTrust) hide the actual donor. The filings show only the sponsor.
- **Schedule B** donor names are not public, so recipients' full funding is unknown. Only grants disclosed by *grantors* appear here.
- **Timing:** 2025 990s and OSF's 2025 grants are incomplete or unpublished. 2026 FEC figures run only to the latest bulk file.
- **FEC totals** are summed from bulk itemized files by cycle, with memo entries excluded. Individual-donor matches by name and city may include namesakes.
- **LDA totals** add expenses and income for self-filing registrants, excluding registrations and amendments. Lobbying through outside firms is not counted.
- **OSF database pages** load in a browser but can return HTTP 403 to automated checkers after repeated requests. The IRS TEOS site also blocks automated checks.
- **Not done in this pass:** a systematic efile.fara.gov query, state-level grants (e.g. California funding to CHIRLA), and an exhaustive earmark search.
