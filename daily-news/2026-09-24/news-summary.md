# Trump administration: top stories, Thursday Sep 24, 2026

*Neutral summary. Times are Mountain Time (MT), with Eastern (ET) where the source uses it.*

## 1. Judge temporarily restores White House press passes for CNN, MS NOW and Politico; reporters back on campus by midday
- Shortly after midnight ET (about 10 p.m. MT Sep 23), U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly, a 2017 Trump appointee, granted a 14-day temporary restraining order. It requires the defendants, **except President Trump**, to "immediately return, reinstate, and restore" the hard passes revoked on or about Sep 18. The ruling rests only on the outlets' Fifth Amendment due-process claim. The First Amendment claims were not decided. The judge said the record gave "little to back up" the national-security rationale and called the "professionalism and decorum" standard "so vague it hardly does the trick." He noted that the government says D.C. Circuit precedent (Sherrill, Karem) was wrongly decided: "Maybe—but this Court is bound by D.C. Circuit precedent, full stop." Order: [CourtListener ECF 24](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754.24.0.pdf) · [docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74823502/cable-news-network-inc-v-trump/)
- Reporters from all three outlets were turned away Thursday morning, and the outlets asked for an emergency hearing, alleging noncompliance ([ECF 26](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754.26.0.pdf)). In a sworn declaration, the White House said staff were told at 7:10 a.m. ET to restore access and that passes were at the gate around 9:55 a.m. ET ([ECF 27](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754/gov.uscourts.dcd.296754.27.0.pdf)). The outlets were back on the grounds around noon ET (about 10 a.m. MT), and the judge then denied the emergency hearing. - The five-network TV pool (ABC, CBS, CNN, Fox, NBC) had suspended pool coverage in solidarity, and no network aired live pool video of the start of the Trump–Xi meeting. The White House launched its own "Trump TV" stream on Sep 21. ## 2. Xi Jinping state visit begins at the White House
Trump welcomed Chinese President Xi for a state summit and dinner. Trump praised a "truly great friendship," and Xi called for "healthy" competition. Treasury Secretary Bessent said the trade truce would be extended to Jan 10. AI ("super intelligence"), Taiwan and the Iran war are on the agenda. Analysts quoted by Reuters expected few breakthroughs. [Reuters via ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/trump-xi-summit-in-washington-begins-with-great-fanfare-but-low-expectations/3052708/) · [AP via Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/24/president-donald-trump-xi-jinping-china/7b94d3be-b811-11f1-94cb-d3d8f22a8c8b_story.html)

## 3. Administration asks Supreme Court to allow third-country deportations
After the 1st Circuit upheld (Sep 18) and then put into effect (Sep 23) a ruling that DHS's third-country removal policy is unlawful, DOJ filed an emergency application on Sep 24 asking the Supreme Court to pause it. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-seek-supreme-court-relief-after-court-blocked-third-country-removals-blanche-2026-09-24/)

## 4. Iowa judge dismisses Trump's suit against the Des Moines Register and pollster Ann Selzer (Sep 23)
Judge Scott Beattie held that the poll and its coverage are protected political speech. Trump's legal team says it will appeal to the Iowa Supreme Court.
