3. Filings made more than 45 days after the trade (STOCK Act) ✓ Verified by SwampForce

Rule: A member must report a covered transaction "not later than 30 days after receiving notification" of it, "but in no case later than 45 days after such transaction." This is the STOCK Act, Pub. L. 112-105, §6 (congress.gov ↗), codified at 5 U.S.C. 13105(l) (uscode.house.gov ↗).

Face-of-filing result: 2,795 transaction lines filed in 2025–2026 were reported more than 45 days after the transaction date shown on the filing. They come from 63 members.

Caveats (neutral):

  1. Some late lines may be dating errors on the form. One Shreve line shows a 3,698-day lag, which is almost certainly a typo.
  2. Some may cover transactions from before the person was a covered official (new members and appointees).
  3. Some may be voluntary disclosures of assets that are not covered.
  4. The House and Senate Ethics Committees assess and waive the $200 fee privately. There is no public list of fees paid.

6. Pardon context (neutral) ✓ Verified by SwampForce

Both members ever convicted of insider trading, Collins (2020) and Buyer (2026), were later pardoned by President Trump. A pardon does not erase the court record of conviction.

Statutes that govern this topic. A statute listed here explains the legal framework. It does not mean anyone violated it; where no charge or finding exists, the note says so.

Pub. L. 112-105 (STOCK Act)5 U.S.C. 13105(l)5 U.S.C. 13106

  • Pub. L. 112-105 (STOCK Act): STOCK Act of 2012. Applied the disclosure law's periodic-transaction reporting to members of Congress.
  • 5 U.S.C. 13105(l): Periodic transaction reports (45-day deadline). Sets the 30/45-day reporting deadline. A late filing is a disclosure violation, not insider trading.
  • 5 U.S.C. 13106: Failure to file or filing false reports. Civil penalty for knowing and willful failure to file; no member has been found in violation in the records reviewed.
