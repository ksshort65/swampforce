People of the State of New York v. Donald J. Trump et al. (civil fraud / Exec. Law § 63(12))

Status: As of September 24, 2026: Appellate Division (Aug. 21, 2025) vacated the ~$464.6 million disgorgement and counsel sanctions but left liability findings and much of the injunctive relief intact via a fractured disposition designed for Court of Appeals review. Cross-appeals / further review in the New York Court of Appeals remain active (defendants' principal brief filed Apr. 2026 seeking reversal of liability/injunctions; AG seeks restoration of monetary relief). Monitor-related injunctive framework remains subject to the stayed/pending high-court posture.
Court
Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County; Appellate Division, First Department; New York Court of Appeals (pending)
Docket
Index No. 452564/2022 (trial); Appeal Nos. 2834–2836; Case Nos. 2023-04925, 2024-01134, 2024-01135 (1st Dept); Court of Appeals review pending after Aug. 21, 2025 Appellate Division decision

Brought by
People of the State of New York, by Letitia James, Attorney General of the State of New York
Filed
2022-09-21 (complaint; AG investigation earlier; Special Proceeding Aug. 2020)
Charges / claims
Civil claims under N.Y. Executive Law § 63(12) for repeated fraudulent/illegal business acts predicated in part on N.Y. Penal Law (falsifying business records / false financial statements / related conspiracy theories). Sought disgorgement, injunctive relief, independent monitor, and independent compliance director.

Outcome. Liability under Exec. Law § 63(12) affirmed by Appellate Division (fractured); entire disgorgement award vacated as an excessive fine (Eighth Amendment); counsel sanctions vacated; Court of Appeals review pending.

  2. 2024-02-16 N.Y. Supreme Court (Engoron, J.)
    Post-trial decision: liability on remaining § 63(12) causes; disgorgement of $363,894,816 plus prejudgment interest totaling $464,576,230.62; extensive injunctive relief and monitor/compliance director.
    iapps.courts.state.ny.us ↗
  3. 2024-02-23 N.Y. Supreme Court (Engoron, J.)
    Judgment entered implementing the post-trial order.
    iapps.courts.state.ny.us ↗
  4. 2025-08-21 Appellate Division, First Department
    ENTERED Aug. 21, 2025: modified judgment on the law to vacate the disgorgement awards in their entirety and vacate counsel sanctions; otherwise affirmed. Fractured panel; Higgitt & Rosado joined the decretal solely to create finality for Court of Appeals review. Moulton (joined by Renwick, P.J.): liability affirmed but disgorgement an excessive fine under the Eighth Amendment. Friedman, J.: would reverse and dismiss; joined vacatur of disgorgement/sanctions.

(concurring in vacatur / dissenting otherwise): would dismiss; wrote that affirming liability while three of five justices would vacate judgment was troubling, and that vacating the monetary award frustrated what he described as the AG's aim. Higgitt, J. (joined by Rosado, J.): joined the decretal solely to ensure finality for Court of Appeals review despite disagreeing with parts of the liability affirmance.

Trump v. Anderson (Colorado ballot / Anderson v. Griswold) and Maine Secretary of State ballot ruling

Status: As of September 24, 2026: Concluded. Supreme Court unanimously reversed Colorado's ballot exclusion (March 4, 2024). Maine Secretary of State withdrew her Section 3 disqualification the same day. Trump appeared on ballots; no further Section 3 state-ballot disqualification litigation of this type remains active against him for the 2024 cycle.
Court
Colorado state courts; Supreme Court of the United States (No. 23-719); Maine Secretary of State
Docket
U.S. Supreme Court No. 23-719; Colorado proceedings captioned Anderson v. Griswold in state courts; Maine SOS challenges (Rosen/Saviello/Strimling et al.)
Official docket
supremecourt.gov ↗; supremecourt.gov ↗; maine.gov ↗
Brought by
Colorado voters (Anderson et al.) under state election procedures; parallel Maine challengers before Secretary of State Shenna Bellows
Filed
2023-09 (Colorado petition ~six months before March 5, 2024 primary); Maine challenges late 2023
Charges / claims
Civil/administrative claims that Section 3 of the Fourteenth Amendment barred Trump from appearing on presidential primary ballots as an oath-breaking insurrectionist.

Outcome. 9–0 reversal of Colorado disqualification; Maine disqualification withdrawn; Trump remained on ballots.

Key rulings, documented issues & sources
  1. 2023-12-19 Colorado Supreme Court
    4–3: ordered Trump excluded from Colorado Republican primary ballot under Section 3 (stayed pending U.S. Supreme Court review).
    supremecourt.gov ↗
  2. 2023-12-28 Maine Secretary of State
    Secretary Bellows ruled Trump's primary petition invalid under Section 3; suspended pending appeal so he remained on the ballot meantime.
    maine.gov ↗
  3. 2024-03-04 U.S. Supreme Court
    Trump v. Anderson, 601 U.S. ___ (2024): per curiam; all nine Justices agreed Colorado could not enforce Section 3 to exclude Trump from the presidential ballot — States lack power to enforce Section 3 against federal candidates; reversed.
    supremecourt.gov ↗
  4. 2024-03-04 Maine Secretary of State
    Modified ruling: withdrew Section 3 disqualification conclusions after Trump v. Anderson; Trump votes counted.
    maine.gov ↗

Documented issues. Supreme Court per curiam: 'Because the Constitution makes Congress, rather than the States, responsible for enforcing Section 3 against federal officeholders and candidates, we reverse.' 'All nine Members of the Court agree with that result.' Opinion warned of a chaotic state-by-state 'patchwork' if States could disqualify presidential candidates.

Sources. supremecourt.gov opinion PDF 23-719; Maine SOS official news releases Dec. 28, 2023 and Mar. 4, 2024.

Trump v. United States (presidential criminal immunity) — related Supreme Court ruling

Status: As of September 24, 2026: Final Supreme Court decision on the books (603 U.S. ___). Remand proceedings in D.D.C. were mooted when the underlying indictment was dismissed without prejudice in Nov.–Dec. 2024.
Court
Supreme Court of the United States
Docket
No. 23-939
Official docket
supremecourt.gov ↗; supremecourt.gov ↗
Brought by
Donald J. Trump (petitioner); United States (respondent) — interlocutory appeal from D.D.C. Jan. 6 prosecution
Filed
Certiorari granted 2024; argued Apr. 25, 2024; decided July 1, 2024
Charges / claims
Not a freestanding prosecution; constitutional question: extent of former-President immunity from criminal prosecution for official acts.

Outcome. Landmark immunity framework announced; underlying D.D.C. case later dismissed without prejudice.

Key rulings, documented issues & sources
  1. 2024-07-01 U.S. Supreme Court
    Held: absolute immunity for actions within conclusive and preclusive constitutional authority; at least presumptive immunity for official acts; no immunity for unofficial acts. Vacated D.C. Circuit judgment and remanded.

Docket 9 of 10

Fischer v. United States (§ 1512(c)(2) obstruction narrowing) — related Supreme Court ruling

Status: As of September 24, 2026: Final Supreme Court decision. Narrowed a frequently charged Jan. 6 obstruction count. Trump v. United States footnote expressly directed the D.D.C. court to reconsider § 1512(c)(2) counts in light of Fischer if necessary (later mooted by dismissal of Trump's D.D.C. case).
Court
Supreme Court of the United States
Docket
No. 23-5572
Official docket
supremecourt.gov ↗; supremecourt.gov ↗
Brought by
Joseph W. Fischer (petitioner Jan. 6 defendant); United States (respondent)
Filed
Argued Apr. 16, 2024; decided June 28, 2024
Charges / claims
Statutory interpretation of 18 U.S.C. § 1512(c)(2) (Sarbanes-Oxley obstruction) as charged against a Jan. 6 defendant; ruling affected charging theories also used in United States v. Trump (D.D.C.).

Outcome. § 1512(c)(2) limited to evidence-impairment theories; many Jan. 6 obstruction counts required reassessment.

Key rulings, documented issues & sources
  1. 2024-06-28 U.S. Supreme Court
    To prove § 1512(c)(2), the government must show the defendant impaired (or attempted to impair) the availability or integrity of records, documents, objects, or other things used in an official proceeding. Vacated D.C. Circuit judgment; remanded.
    supremecourt.gov ↗

Documented issues. Roberts, C.J., for the Court: (c)(2) is not a general obstruction catch-all; it is tethered to (c)(1)'s evidence-impairment focus. Barrett, J., dissented (joined by Sotomayor & Kagan), arguing the majority atextually narrowed a broad statute.

Sources. supremecourt.gov slip opinion 23-5572.
