Why there is no single "grand total" of fraud

The five categories below measure different things, and they overlap. Adding them together would double count.

CategoryWhat it measuresIs it fraud?
Improper payments (OMB/agency estimates)Payments that should not have been made, were the wrong amount, or could not be documented ("unknown")Not all fraud. This includes underpayments, paperwork and documentation errors, and eligibility errors. Agencies do not break out the fraud share.
GAO fraud estimateA statistical estimate of direct annual losses to fraudEstimated fraud. It partly overlaps improper payments and excludes revenue (tax) fraud.
IG questioned costs / funds put to better useAudit recommendationsNot losses. These amounts may never be disallowed or realized.
IG investigative recoveries / DOJ False Claims ActMoney ordered or recovered through enforcementDocumented enforcement outcomes. They overlap each other, because OIG investigations feed DOJ cases.
DOGE savingsClaimed cancellationsNot a fraud measure. GAO found the claims largely unverifiable (see below).

Method used here: the tally keeps separate running totals by tier and never sums across tiers.

  • Tier 1 (measured error, agency-estimated): improper payments, about $3 trillion cumulative from FY2003 to FY2025 (GAO). The OMB dataset sum for FY2004–FY2025 is $2,984.0B (the yearly rows below, each rounded to $0.1B, add up to $2,983.9B). The FY2025 figure is $186B.
  • Tier 2 (estimated fraud): GAO puts fraud at $233B–$521B per year (based on FY2018–2022 data). Estimated pandemic fraud: SBA OIG found more than $200B potentially fraudulent in PPP and EIDL, and GAO estimates $100B–$135B in UI. Those two cover different programs, so the combined pandemic figure is about $300B–$335B, estimated or potential, not adjudicated.
  • Tier 3 (enforcement outcomes): FY2025 DOJ False Claims Act recoveries were $6.8B. CIGIE reported FY2025 investigative receivables and recoveries of $20.6B. These two overlap, so they are not added.
  • Tier 4 (claims): DOGE claimed $215B. GAO could not verify most of it.

2. GAO's government-wide fraud estimate ✓ Verified by SwampForce

GAO-24-105833 (Apr 2024) estimates that the federal government loses $233 billion to $521 billion a year to fraud, based on FY2018–FY2022 data. The estimate excludes revenue fraud. It is a statistical estimate, not a count of proven cases, and it overlaps improper payments. gao.gov ↗

4. DOJ False Claims Act recoveries ✓ Verified by SwampForce

FY2025: more than $6.8B in settlements and judgments, a record. Of that, $5.7B related to health care and $5.3B came from qui tam (whistleblower) cases. 1,297 qui tam suits were filed. Total recoveries since 1986 exceed $85B. justice.gov ↗

Statute: 31 U.S.C. 3729–3733. uscode.house.gov ↗

5. Pandemic program fraud ✓ Verified by SwampForce

  • SBA OIG Report 23-09 (June 27, 2023): more than $200B potentially fraudulent, made up of EIDL $136B and PPP $64B. That is about 17% of roughly $1.2T disbursed. About $30B in EIDL funds was seized or returned. "Potentially fraudulent" is the OIG's term and is not an adjudicated amount. oversight.gov ↗
  • GAO-23-106696 (Sept 2023): estimated UI fraud during the pandemic was likely $100B–$135B. States themselves reported finding $5.3B in fraudulent overpayments and recovering $1.2B of it. gao.gov ↗
  • GAO-23-105523: $878B in UI benefits was paid from Apr 2020 to Sep 2022. gao.gov ↗
  • GAO-23-106556: at least 1,044 people convicted of pandemic program fraud as of Jan 2023. gao.gov ↗
  • Pandemic Response Accountability Committee portal: pandemicoversight.gov ↗

6. DOGE savings claims versus the GAO audit ✓ Verified by SwampForce

GAO-26-108615 (Aug 6, 2026), files.gao.gov ↗:

Show the full list (6 items)Hide the list
  • DOGE's website claimed about $215B in savings (unaudited). Its itemized "Wall of Receipts" totaled $110.34B: contracts $61.02B, grants $49.21B, leases $113M.
  • Contracts: only 27.5% ($16.8B) of claimed contract savings followed DOGE's own stated method. $27.4B was claimed on contracts where no termination action was taken. Examples: a DHA contract claimed at $1.7B saved had no action taken, and an Air Force BIM contract was claimed at $4B against a $3.75B value reduction.
  • Grants: GAO could not verify 96% ($47.32B) of claimed grant savings.
  • Leases: DOGE claimed $113M, but its own entries sum to $53.5M, and GAO calculated $31.8M. That is an $81.1M overstatement. 108 of the leases were already being terminated before DOGE existed.
  • The DOGE temporary organization ended July 4, 2026. Its website was last updated Jan 1, 2026.
  • Verdict on the headline DOGE savings figure: Disproven as stated. GAO found that the itemized claims were not supported at the amounts claimed. Some real savings exist (at least $16.8B in contract savings computed on DOGE's method), but no audited total is available.

Claims the record does not support ✓ Verified by SwampForce

  • "Improper payments = fraud": Disproven. OMB and GAO define improper payments to include underpayments and documentation errors.
  • Adding improper payments, the GAO fraud estimate and IG and DOJ recoveries into one "waste and fraud" number is double counting.
  • DOGE's "$215B saved" / "$110B receipts": not supported by GAO's audit at the claimed amounts.

Statutes that govern this topic. A statute listed here explains the legal framework. It does not mean anyone violated it; where no charge or finding exists, the note says so.

31 U.S.C. 3351-3358 (PIIA)31 U.S.C. 3729-3733

  • 31 U.S.C. 3351-3358 (PIIA): Improper payments reporting. Requires agencies to estimate improper payments; improper is not the same as fraudulent.
  • 31 U.S.C. 3729-3733: False Claims Act. Civil recoveries for fraud on the government; qui tam under 3730.
