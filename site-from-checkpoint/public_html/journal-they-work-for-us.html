The settlements record

What the $17.2 million is

The Office of Compliance (renamed the Office of Congressional Workplace Rights in 2018) released the figure on Nov 16, 2017: 264 awards and settlements, $17,240,854, fiscal years 1997 through 2017, paid from its Treasury account. It says a large share came from legislative-branch offices other than the House or Senate, and involved laws such as the overtime rules of the Fair Labor Standards Act, the Family and Medical Leave Act and the Americans with Disabilities Act. It is not a count of member sexual-misconduct cases. Office of Compliance memo, Nov 16, 2017 ↗ OCWR page ↗

The 2018 law

The Congressional Accountability Act of 1995 Reform Act (S. 3749) became Public Law 115-397 on Dec 21, 2018. Members must reimburse the Treasury for harassment awards and settlements, and those are referred to the Ethics Committee. CAA Reform Act, Pub. L. 115-397 (congress.gov) ↗

The votes

Mar 4, 2026: on Rep. Nancy Mace’s H.Res. 1100, the House voted 357–65 (1 present) to refer it to the Ethics Committee instead of adopting it. Republicans 175–38, Democrats 182–27. House Roll Call 83, Mar 4, 2026 ↗ H.Res. 1100 (congress.gov) ↗

Jun 30, 2026: Rep. Thomas Massie’s H.Res. 1399 passed 420–0. Republicans 209 yea, 1 present (Mace); Democrats 210 yea; one independent yea. It directed the Ethics Committee and the OCWR to publish, within 60 days, the names of members in taxpayer-paid sexual-harassment cases and several dollar totals. House Roll Call 233, Jun 30, 2026 ↗ H.Res. 1399 (congress.gov) ↗

What came back

The Ethics Committee said on Jul 2 that it does not handle settlements and has not been notified of any member sexual-misconduct award or settlement since the 2018 law. House Ethics Committee statement, Jul 2, 2026 ↗ The OCWR answered on Aug 31, 2026:
