Documented Deception of American Voters

129 cases verified by Swamp Force against the original record: 65 from the first term and 64 from 2021 to the present. 69 are proven false and 60 are rated misleading. 79 are settled by the official record.

The PDF brief and PDF appendix were printed before the Sep 24, 2026 re-verification and still count every catalog row. The figures on this page are the verified ones.

Key findings

  • 129 claims about President Trump or his administration were checked by us against the original record and found false or misleading.
  • 108 were never corrected by whoever pushed them (49 proven false, 59 misleading). A later fact-check addressed 101 of them; the other 7 have no fact-check on file, only the record.
  • 131 more were rated false by a fact-checker and are still being checked; they are not counted here.
  • 79 are settled by the official record: a court, the DOJ, an inspector general, the FEC or government data.

The law in brief

The First Amendment protects much false speech: United States v. Alvarez (2012) struck down the Stolen Valor Act, and the plurality wrote that "falsity alone may not suffice to bring the speech outside the First Amendment." A public official suing over a falsehood about official conduct must prove "actual malice," meaning knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard of the truth (New York Times Co. v. Sullivan, 1964). The Speech or Debate Clause (art. I, § 6) protects legislative acts. It did not cover a senator's private publication of the Pentagon Papers (Gravel v. United States, 1972) or a senator's newsletters and press release (Hutchinson v. Proxmire, 1979). Each House may discipline its own members (art. I, § 5).

Our view · Opinion

Congress writes its own rules, and it should police its own members' false public statements. The full argument is in The Great American Betrayal.
