Methodology

How we picked our fact-checkers

We tested 16 fact-checkers against the same 7 tests. 1 was approved, 10 were approved with caution, and 5 were rejected. Evidence date: Sep 24, 2026.

The rule: a fact-checker is never our proof

Every case on Fake News Exposed is first checked by us against the original record: the transcript, the video, the court filing, the government data, or the outlet's own correction. Only after that check does a case get the label Verified by SwampForce.

An approved fact-checker can then be named as a second confirmation (Also confirmed by). It is never the proof, and a checker never confirms a case about its own parent outlet. Of the 129 verified cases, 94 also carry a confirmation from an approved checker; the other 35 rest on our own check alone. Cases that a fact-checker rated false but that we have not yet confirmed against the original record are listed as Still being checked and are not counted.

The 7 tests (the same for every checker)

Show the full list (7 items)Hide the list
  1. IFCN status. Is it a current signatory of the International Fact-Checking Network's code of principles? Current passes; lapsed, in renewal or never means caution. Status alone never approves or rejects.
  2. A published corrections policy. Required. If we could not locate one, the checker is rejected. That is a documentation failure, not a finding about its accuracy.
  3. A public corrections log. If we could not locate one, that means caution.
  4. Its record of corrections and reversals. Open corrections are expected. A documented reversal of a politically charged rating, or an integrity failure, means caution.
  5. Funding transparency. Funding that is not itemized, or not disclosed for the fact-check unit, means caution.
  6. A published methodology. Required. If we could not locate one, the checker is rejected.
  7. Still operating. A closed or leaderless unit is archive-only (caution), or rejected if its policies can no longer be checked.

Approved means it passed all 7 with no caution. Approved with caution means it has a corrections policy and a methodology we could locate, but hit one or more caution tests. Rejected means we could not locate its corrections policy or its methodology.

When we say we "could not locate" a page, that means our search did not find it on Sep 24, 2026. It is not a claim that none exists. A rejected checker can be re-tested if the pages are found.

So it is approved only as secondary confirmation after our own primary-source verification.
