## Plain-English findings

### Documented by primary records
1. **Federal officials pushed platforms to act on COVID content in 2021.**
   - White House emails in the court record include Rob Flaherty to Facebook, Jul 15, 2021: "Are you guys fucking serious? I want an answer on what happened here and I want it today." (W.D. La. ruling p.23)
   - The White House press secretary said on the record, "We're flagging problematic posts for Facebook." (Psaki, Jul 15, 2021)
   - The Surgeon General's advisory urged platforms to "Impose clear consequences for accounts that repeatedly violate platform policies."
   - The CDC held "BOLO" meetings with platforms. (p.47)
2. **Both big companies later said so in writing.**
   - Zuckerberg to Congress (Aug 26, 2024): officials "repeatedly pressured our teams for months to censor certain COVID-19 content, including humor and satire." He added: "I believe the government pressure was wrong."
   - Alphabet (Sep 23, 2025): officials "pressed the Company regarding certain user-generated content ... that did not violate its policies."
   - **Both companies also say the final decisions were their own.**
3. **A Facebook internal email ties one policy to pressure.** Asked why Facebook had removed claims that COVID was man-made, staff told Nick Clegg (Jul 14, 2021): "Because we were under pressure from the administration and others to do more ... We shouldn't have done it." This is a subpoenaed document reproduced in the House Judiciary majority report.
4. **Facebook removed "man-made" origin claims from Feb 8 to May 26, 2021, then stopped.**
   - Official intelligence assessments since then are split. None has settled the question.
   - The FBI and CIA lean toward a lab origin at low-to-moderate confidence; the CIA position is reported.
   - The 2021 ODNI assessment calls both origins "plausible."
5. **Platforms acted against the physician plaintiffs.** This is documented in company records made public:
   - Twitter put Dr. Jay Bhattacharya on a "Trends Blacklist" (Twitter Files #2).
   - Twitter labeled a Dr. Martin Kulldorff tweet "Misleading" (Twitter Files #10).
   - The internal screenshots do not show a government request behind these two actions.
6. **Stanford-led projects routed flagged content to platforms, with ties to the government.**
   - The Election Integrity Partnership was formed "in consultation with CISA." It reports that platforms labeled, removed, or soft-blocked 35% of the URLs it shared.
   - The Virality Project reports "strong ties" with the Surgeon General's office and CDC. It says platforms acted "in accordance with their policies."
7. **The Trump suspensions are documented in the companies' own words.**
   - Twitter (Jan 8, 2021) cited "the risk of further incitement of violence."
   - The Oversight Board (May 5, 2021) upheld Facebook's restriction but called an indefinite suspension "indeterminate and standardless."
   - Twitter Files #5 shows staff had earlier written: "I think we'd have a hard time saying this is incitement."
8. **The court case ended in a limited settlement, not a merits ruling.**
   - Consent decree, SO ORDERED Mar 25, 2026: for 10 years, the Surgeon General, CDC, and CISA may not threaten five platforms to suppress the plaintiffs' protected speech.
   - It expressly is **not** an admission of liability (¶17).

### What the courts actually held (and did not hold)
- **District court (Jul 4, 2023): preliminary findings only.** Judge Doughty found plaintiffs "likely" to succeed. His "most massive attack against free speech" line opens with "If the allegations made by Plaintiffs are true."
- **Fifth Circuit (Sep 8 and Oct 3, 2023): also preliminary.** It found that the White House, Surgeon General, FBI, CDC, and (on rehearing) CISA "likely coerced or significantly encouraged" platform decisions.
- **Supreme Court (Jun 26, 2024, 6–3): no merits ruling.**
  - It reversed on **standing only**: "We therefore lack jurisdiction to reach the merits."
  - It said many district findings "unfortunately appear to be clearly erroneous" (fn.4).
  - Alito's dissent called the conduct "blatantly unconstitutional." That is a dissent, not a holding.
- **Aug 26, 2024: everything preliminary was wiped.** The Fifth Circuit vacated the injunction entirely.
- **Result:** no court has issued a final ruling that any official violated the First Amendment in this matter.

### Alleged or asserted, not established by primary record
- Zuckerberg's Rogan remarks (Jan 10, 2025) are his own claims, not tied to a document:
  - Officials were "threatening repercussions."
  - The government wanted "anything that says that vaccines might have side effects" taken down.
  - He said he "wasn't involved in those conversations directly."
- Characterizations in the House Judiciary **majority-staff** reports, such as "unconstitutional" and "colluded," are the committee majority's conclusions. The minority (Raskin) says 20 Alphabet employees testified to **no** coercion.
- EO 14149 (Jan 20, 2025) says the prior administration "trampled free speech rights." That is the executive's characterization, not a court finding.

### Popularly believed but not supported by the record
- **"The FBI or government ordered the laptop story suppressed."** Not shown.
  - Roth testified under oath that Hunter Biden was raised in FBI meetings "but not by the government, to the best of my recollection."
  - Taibbi wrote: "no evidence - that I've seen - of any government involvement in the laptop story."
  - What is documented: general FBI hack-and-leak warnings, plus the FBI declining to comment when asked. The district court called that "significant encouragement" (p.107), but the finding was preliminary and later vacated.
- **"The Twitter Files show the Biden White House ordering takedowns in 2020."** In 2020 the "Biden team" was a campaign. Twitter Files #1 says requests from "both the Trump White House and the Biden campaign were received and honored."
- **"The Supreme Court ruled on the censorship" (either way).** It did not reach the merits.
- **"Collins and Fauci got the Great Barrington Declaration censored."** Collins's FOIA'd email asks for "a quick and devastating published take down of its premises," which means a published rebuttal. The Fifth Circuit found NIAID not liable.
- **"Doctors lost licenses just for speaking."**
  - The Washington order on Dr. Ryan Cole *restricted* his license, citing both his statements and deficient patient care.
  - California's AB 2098 was enjoined, then repealed, and no enforcement was found.
  - No primary record reviewed shows a revocation for speech alone.
- **"Networks censoring the President" (Nov 5, 2020).**
  - The full address was carried unedited by C-SPAN and the White House, and the official transcript exists.
  - The cutaways were decisions by private broadcasters; no government actor was involved.
  - The cutaways themselves are reported only.

## 1. Murthy v. Missouri (formerly Missouri v. Biden)

**Plaintiffs:** Missouri, Louisiana, Drs. Bhattacharya, Kulldorff, and Kheriaty, Jim Hoft, and Jill Hines. Filed May 5, 2022 (ruling p.133).

**District court, Jul 4, 2023 (ECF 293, 155 pp.)**
- **p.2:** "If the allegations made by Plaintiffs are true, the present case arguably involves the most massive attack against free speech in United States' history." Also: "the censorship alleged in this case almost exclusively targeted conservative speech."
- **p.9 (White House):**
  - Flaherty, Feb 6, 2021, on a parody account of Biden's granddaughter: "Cannot stress the degree to which this needs to be resolved immediately."
  - Twitter suspended the account within about 45 minutes.
  - The Supreme Court (fn.4) later said this impersonation request was mischaracterized as a censorship request.
- **p.12 (White House / Surgeon General):**
  - A Meta executive copied Slavitt on emails to Murthy about "expanded penalties" (May 28–Jul 10, 2021).
  - Meta wrote: "We think there is considerably more we can do in 'partnership' with you and your team to drive behavior."
- **p.23:** Flaherty, Jul 15, 2021: "Are you guys fucking serious? I want an answer on what happened here and I want it today."
- **p.24:** Biden, Jul 16: "[T]hey're killing people." On Jul 20, Bedingfield raised Section 230 liability.
- **p.47 (CDC):**
  - Carol Crawford set up "COVID BOLO" meetings; the first was May 14, 2021.
  - Census Bureau staff helped prepare the slides.
- **p.53 (NIAID):** Fauci "does not specifically recall" communicating with platforms.
- **pp.62–63 (FBI):**
  - Hack-and-leak warnings to platforms before the 2020 election.
  - Chan denies urging policy changes, but admits repeatedly asking whether platforms had changed their hacked-materials policies.
  - Dehmlow declined to comment when Facebook asked about the laptop, although the FBI had held it since Dec 2019.
- **p.68 (CISA):**
  - "Switchboarding," per Scully: forwarding election officials' flags to platforms, which decided under their own policies.
  - It stopped in 2022.
- **p.107:** "The FBI's failure to alert social-media companies that the Hunter Biden laptop story was real … is particularly troubling … demonstrative of significant encouragement by the FBI Defendants."
- **p.131:** "Specific instances of censorship substantially likely to be the direct result of Government involvement are too numerous to fully detail."
- **p.154:** "the United States Government seems to have assumed a role similar to an Orwellian 'Ministry of Truth.'"
- **p.155:** Motion granted in part, denied in part. Class certification denied.

**Injunction (ECF 294)**
- Enjoined parties included:
  - HHS and NIAID; the Surgeon General; CDC (named officials); Census
  - FBI (Dehmlow, Chan); DOJ
  - White House officials; CISA (Easterly, Scully); DHS
  - The State Department's GEC
- Ten prohibitions, including flagging content and "switchboarding" through EIP, the Virality Project, and SIO.
- Carve-outs for crime, national security, and foreign influence.
- Denied as to FDA, Treasury, EAC, and others.

**Fifth Circuit, Sep 8, 2023**
- **p.42:** "the White House, acting in concert with the Surgeon General's office, likely (1) coerced the platforms … and (2) significantly encouraged the platforms' decisions."
- **pp.54–56:** FBI likely coerced and significantly encouraged.
- **pp.57–58:** CDC "not plainly coercive" but "likely significantly encouraged."
- **pp.59–60:** NIAID and the State Department not liable.

**Fifth Circuit on rehearing, Oct 3, 2023**
- **pp.59–61:** Adds CISA as the FBI's "primary facilitator," which "likely significantly encouraged." The five entities found: White House, Surgeon General, CDC, FBI, CISA.
- **p.74:** Affirmed as to those five; everything else reversed. Only a modified prohibition #6 was kept.

**Supreme Court, Jun 26, 2024 (Barrett, 6–3)**
- **Held:** "Neither the individual nor the state plaintiffs have established Article III standing."
- **p.13:** "We begin—and end—with standing … We therefore lack jurisdiction to reach the merits."
- **fn.4:** Lower-court findings "unfortunately appear to be clearly erroneous."
- Jill Hines made "the best showing," but Facebook targeted her before most of the White House and CDC contacts.
- **Alito dissent (with Thomas and Gorsuch):** "one of the most important free speech cases to reach this Court in years." Also: "blatantly unconstitutional … If a coercive campaign is carried out with enough sophistication, it may get by."

**After the Supreme Court ruling**
- **Aug 26, 2024:** the Fifth Circuit vacated the injunction entirely and remanded.
- **Nov 8, 2024:** Doughty allowed jurisdictional discovery (ECF 404).
- **Jan 20, 2025:** EO 14149 was signed; the consent decree cites it.
- **Mar 25, 2026: consent decree (ECF 478), SO ORDERED by Doughty.**
  - Binds the Surgeon General, CDC, and CISA for 10 years.
  - Covers only the plaintiffs' content, on Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, and YouTube.
  - Bars threats of "adverse legal, regulatory, or economic government sanction" to get protected speech removed.
  - Expressly allows officials to say posts are "inaccurate, wrong."
  - ¶17: no admission of liability.
  - DOJ and NCLA issued statements; see sources.

**What happened to the physician plaintiffs**
- **Jay Bhattacharya:** confirmed NIH Director Mar 25, 2025, **53–47** (Senate Roll Call #141; cloture #140).
- **Martin Kulldorff:** chaired ACIP in 2025. Named HHS ASPE chief science officer Dec 1, 2025 (HHS release). Per NCLA, Bhattacharya and Kulldorff withdrew from the case on joining government.
- **Aaron Kheriaty:** party to the 2026 settlement (NCLA). His UC Irvine dismissal over the vaccine mandate and his later suit are reported, not confirmed by primary record.

## 2. Zuckerberg and Meta

**Letter to Chairman Jordan, Aug 26, 2024** (signed scan; also posted by @JudiciaryGOP)
- "In 2021, senior officials from the Biden Administration, including the White House, repeatedly pressured our teams for months to censor certain COVID-19 content, including humor and satire, and expressed a lot of frustration with our teams when we didn't agree."
- "Ultimately, it was our decision whether or not to take content down, and we own our decisions."
- "I believe the government pressure was wrong, and I regret that we were not more outspoken about it."
- "we should not compromise our content standards due to pressure from any Administration in either direction."
- On the laptop: "the FBI warned us about a potential Russian disinformation operation about the Biden family and Burisma … when we saw a New York Post story … we sent that story to fact-checkers for review and temporarily demoted it … It's since been made clear that the reporting was not Russian disinformation, and in retrospect, we shouldn't have demoted the story."
- **Note:** the letter describes a *general* FBI warning. It does not say the FBI named the Post story.

**Meta, "More Speech and Fewer Mistakes," Jan 7, 2025**
- "Starting in the US, we are ending our third party fact-checking program and moving to a Community Notes model."
- "Too much harmless content gets censored."

## 4. Physicians and scientists

**Great Barrington Declaration (Oct 4, 2020) and the Collins email**
- Collins's email (Oct 8, 2020, FOIA release): "This proposal from the three fringe epidemiologists … There needs to be a quick and devastating published take down of its premises. I don't see anything like that on line yet – is it underway?"
- The email says "Mike Leavitt," as written; this means Michael Levitt.
- It calls for a published rebuttal, not removal.

**Platform removals**
- Twitter: Bhattacharya "Trends Blacklist" (TF#2); Kulldorff "Misleading" label (TF#10).
- YouTube removed the Mar 18, 2021 DeSantis roundtable. The plaintiffs' allegation is recorded at ruling p.5. YouTube's statement that the claims "contradict the consensus of local and global health authorities" is reported (NBC).
- Alphabet 2025 ¶23: YouTube terminated channels under its COVID policy through 2024.

**California**
- **AB 2098** (Ch. 938, signed Sep 30, 2022) made COVID "misinformation or disinformation" by physicians unprofessional conduct (B&P §2270), effective Jan 1, 2023.
- **Høeg v. Newsom:** Judge Shubb, Jan 25, 2023, granted a preliminary injunction on Fourteenth Amendment **vagueness** grounds. The term "scientific consensus" was ambiguous, and he did not reach the First Amendment. The same order notes that a C.D. Cal. court denied a similar motion (McDonald v. Lawson).
- **SB 815** (Ch. 294, signed Sep 30, 2023) repealed §2270, effective Jan 1, 2024.

**Medical boards**
- **FSMB, Jul 2021:** physicians spreading COVID vaccine misinformation "are risking disciplinary action by state medical boards, including the suspension or revocation of their medical license." Quoted in FSMB's 2022 report.
- **Washington Medical Commission v. Ryan Cole, Final Order Jan 2024:**
  - The license was restricted.
  - Findings covered his public statements ("Knowingly false statements or those made in reckless disregard of the truth erode the public's trust") and deficient care for four patients.
- **Maine v. Meryl Nass (suspended Jan 2022):** reported, not confirmed by primary record; the order URL returned 403.

**Lab-leak labeling vs. later assessments**
- Facebook removed "man-made" claims from Feb 8 to May 26, 2021 (company record).
- Its internal email attributes the policy to "pressure from the administration and others."
- **ODNI 2021:**
  - Both hypotheses are "plausible."
  - Four IC elements plus the NIC assessed natural origin with low confidence.
  - One element assessed lab origin with moderate confidence.
- **FBI:** Director Wray on Fox News, Feb 28, 2023, said the origin was "most likely a potential lab incident." These are his own words on TV; no FBI document was located.
- **CIA, Jan 2025:** low confidence that a research-related origin is more likely. Reported; no cia.gov document found.
- **DOE:** low-confidence lab assessment, reported.
- **White House lab-leak page (Apr 2025):** government advocacy, not a new intelligence product.

## 5. Who was behind it: officials and organizations

- **White House:** Flaherty, Slavitt, Psaki, Bedingfield, and Biden. Quotes and page cites are in `people.csv`.
  - Psaki, Jul 15, 2021: "We're flagging problematic posts for Facebook … there's about 12 people who are producing 65 percent of anti-vaccine misinformation."
  - Psaki, Jul 16, 2021: "we're in regular touch with social media platforms … You all make decisions, just like the social media platforms make decisions." She also said: "You shouldn't be banned from one platform and not others."
- **Biden:**
  - Jul 16, 2021: "They're killing people" (PBS raw video).
  - Jul 19, 2021: "Facebook isn't killing people; these 12 people who are out there giving misinformation—anyone listening to it is getting hurt by it. It's killing people." (official transcript)
- **Surgeon General advisory, Jul 15, 2021:** "Prioritize early detection of misinformation 'super-spreaders' and repeat offenders."
  - Its definition of misinformation explicitly covers misleading claims, including a *true* anecdote that is "highly misleading."
  - It also warns against "conflating controversial or unorthodox claims with misinformation."
- **CDC:** BOLO meetings (p.47). The Virality Project cites CDC and Surgeon General "bidirectional situational awareness" (VP p.17).
- **FBI FITF:** Chan and Dehmlow; hack-and-leak warnings (pp.62–63, p.107).
- **CISA:** "switchboarding" (p.68). The House majority report ("The Weaponization of CISA," Jun 26, 2023) calls it censorship; that is the committee majority's characterization.
- **Stanford Internet Observatory**
  - **EIP, "The Long Fuse" (2021):**
    - 639 in-scope tickets; 72% concerned delegitimizing the election.
    - 16% of tickets were filed by the Center for Internet Security, a nonprofit.
    - "35% of the URLs we shared with Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, and YouTube were either labeled, removed, or soft blocked."
  - **Virality Project (2022):**
    - 911 tickets.
    - The Surgeon General's office "incorporated VP's research and perspectives into its own vaccine misinformation strategy."
    - Platforms acted "in accordance with their policies."
- **House Judiciary majority-staff reports** (label: majority-staff congressional reports)
  - "The Weaponization of CISA" (Jun 26, 2023).
  - "The Censorship-Industrial Complex" (May 1, 2024), with an appendix of subpoenaed documents. It includes Amazon: the "impetus for this request is criticism from the Biden Administration" (books, Mar 2021).
