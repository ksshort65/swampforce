Estimate, not a finding. On Dec 18, 2025, First Assistant U.S. Attorney Joe Thompson said Minnesota's 14 "high-risk" Medicaid programs had paid out $18B since 2018 and that "half or more" (about $9B) could be fraudulent (MPR: mprnews.org ↗; AP: apnews.com ↗). No court or audit has adopted that figure. It is labeled Unresolved (no official finding) and kept out of every running total. At the time, the amounts tied to actual prosecutions were roughly $300M.

Methodology and caveats

  • Charged and convicted counts use the figures DOJ publishes. FOF defendant #79 (Fahima Mahamud) was charged in both FOF and CCAP; she is counted once, and her CCAP dollars are listed separately from her FOF dollars.
  • Amounts mix "claimed" and "paid" bases because that is how DOJ reports them. Both bases are shown where DOJ gives both.
  • The list is not exhaustive. It covers the program-fraud waves DOJ has grouped as Minnesota fraud. Stand-alone PPP, EIDL, and unemployment-insurance prosecutions in D. Minn. are not included.
  • State record: the Minnesota Office of the Legislative Auditor (June 13, 2024) found that the Minnesota Department of Education's inadequate oversight of Feeding Our Future created opportunities for fraud: auditor.leg.state.mn.us ↗ (PDF: auditor.leg.state.mn.us ↗).
  • Governing statutes in these cases: 18 U.S.C. 1343 (wire fraud), 18 U.S.C. 1347 / 1349 (health care fraud and conspiracy), 18 U.S.C. 1956 / 1957 (money laundering), 18 U.S.C. 666 (federal program bribery; used in FOF), 18 U.S.C. 371 (conspiracy). Examples: the FOF trial brief charges 18 U.S.C. 371 and 1343 (storage.courtlistener.com ↗).

2. "Money to Somalia / al-Shabaab": what the record shows ✓ Verified by SwampForce

Documented in charging documents or DOJ releases (Proven that the allegation was made in a court filing; proven as fact only where a conviction covers it)

Show the full list (6 items)Hide the list
  1. Kenya transfers (FOF, "Empire" group). The USAO release on Sept 4, 2025 says Abdiaziz Farah (convicted, sentenced to 28 years) "sent millions of dollars in fraud proceeds abroad." Among other things, his brother in Kenya said he had received "$1,287,000" in the days before Dec 27, 2021. The release describes a $300,000 transfer labeled "family support," an apartment building in Nairobi, and land in Mandera, Kenya. The brother, Ahmednaji Sheikh, was charged with conspiracy to commit international money laundering (allegation): irs.gov ↗
  2. EIDBI. Abdinajib Yussuf allegedly "sent more than $200,000 in fraud proceeds to Kenya" (USAO, Dec 18, 2025, IRS-CI mirror above).
  3. HSS. Asad Adow allegedly used proceeds "to invest in real estate in Kenya" (USAO, Sept 18, 2025). Pristine Health defendants allegedly spent proceeds on travel to London, Sydney, Dubai, Istanbul, and Saudi Arabia (USAO, Dec 18, 2025).
  4. Treasury, official statements. On Jan 9, 2026, FinCEN issued four notices of investigation to Minnesota money services businesses, a Geographic Targeting Order (reports on transfers of $3,000 or more to beneficiaries outside the U.S. from banks and transmitters in Hennepin and Ramsey counties), and an alert on child-nutrition fraud. Treasury said law enforcement "has limited insight into the persons located abroad who receive the proceeds": home.treasury.gov ↗; GTO: federalregister.gov ↗. The GTO was renewed Aug 7, 2026, with Treasury saying "a portion of which has been laundered overseas." Neither release names Somalia or al-Shabaab: home.treasury.gov ↗; federalregister.gov ↗
  5. No terrorism charges. No defendant in these fraud cases has been charged under 18 U.S.C. 2339B (material support to a designated FTO) or 18 U.S.C. 2339C (terrorism financing), based on every DOJ release reviewed.
  6. Earlier state record. Minnesota's Legislative Auditor (March 2019) was "unable to substantiate the allegation that individuals in Minnesota sent CCAP fraud money to a foreign country where a terrorist organization obtained and used it": lrl.mn.gov ↗

Neutral note. Most FOF, HSS, and EIDBI defendants are from Minnesota's Somali community, and the community was also victimized by these schemes (CBS News reporting). This page describes individual defendants, not an ethnic group, and attributes no crime to anyone who has not been charged.

Statutes that govern this topic. A statute listed here explains the legal framework. It does not mean anyone violated it; where no charge or finding exists, the note says so.

18 U.S.C. 1343; 18 U.S.C. 134918 U.S.C. 195618 U.S.C. 66618 U.S.C. 2339B31 U.S.C. 5326

  • 18 U.S.C. 1343; 18 U.S.C. 1349: Feeding Our Future / HSS / EIDBI charges. Wire fraud and conspiracy: primary charges in the Minnesota cases.
  • 18 U.S.C. 1956: Money laundering. Charged in Feeding Our Future cases including international transfers.
  • 18 U.S.C. 666: Federal program bribery. Charged in some FOF cases (kickbacks).
  • 18 U.S.C. 2339B: Material support to FTO (not charged). No Minnesota fraud defendant charged under this section; al-Shabaab funding remains Unresolved.
  • 31 U.S.C. 5326: Geographic Targeting Order. FinCEN authority for the Hennepin/Ramsey County money-transfer GTO.
