# Minnesota program fraud: federal charges 2022–2026, and the "money to Somalia" question

*Compiled Sep 24, 2026 (MT). Data file: `minnesota-fraud.csv`. Every amount below is DOJ's alleged figure unless it is marked as proven through a conviction. An indictment is an allegation, and defendants are presumed innocent unless convicted.*

**Access note:** justice.gov (USAO-MN pages) blocks automated fetches from this research box (Akamai challenge). DOJ Office of Public Affairs pages loaded and are cited directly. For USAO-MN releases we cite the verbatim IRS-CI mirror on irs.gov, which reproduces the USAO text, or the HHS-OIG mirror. All cited URLs returned HTTP 200 on Sept 24, 2026, except where the table says otherwise.

**Estimate, not a finding.** On Dec 18, 2025, First Assistant U.S. Attorney Joe Thompson said Minnesota's 14 "high-risk" Medicaid programs had paid out $18B since 2018 and that "half or more" (about $9B) could be fraudulent (MPR: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/18/minnesota-fraud-new-charges-medicaid-scam ; AP: https://apnews.com/article/minnesota-fraud-charges-fbad68312012dc02a4060852474f72ee). No court or audit has adopted that figure. Label it **Unresolved (no official finding)** and keep it out of any running total. At the time, the amounts tied to actual prosecutions were roughly $300M.

**Methodology and caveats**
- Charged and convicted counts use the figures DOJ publishes. FOF defendant #79 (Fahima Mahamud) was charged in both FOF and CCAP; she is counted once, and her CCAP dollars are listed separately from her FOF dollars.
- Amounts mix "claimed" and "paid" bases because that is how DOJ reports them. Both bases are shown where DOJ gives both.
- The list is not exhaustive. It covers the program-fraud waves DOJ has grouped as Minnesota fraud. Stand-alone PPP, EIDL, and unemployment-insurance prosecutions in D. Minn. are not included.
- **State record:** the Minnesota Office of the Legislative Auditor (June 13, 2024) found that the Minnesota Department of Education's inadequate oversight of Feeding Our Future created opportunities for fraud: https://www.auditor.leg.state.mn.us/sreview/2024/mdefof.htm (PDF: https://www.auditor.leg.state.mn.us/sreview/pdf/2024-mdefof.pdf).
- Governing statutes in these cases: **18 U.S.C. 1343** (wire fraud), **18 U.S.C. 1347 / 1349** (health care fraud and conspiracy), **18 U.S.C. 1956 / 1957** (money laundering), **18 U.S.C. 666** (federal program bribery; used in FOF), **18 U.S.C. 371** (conspiracy). Examples: the FOF trial brief charges 18 U.S.C. 371 and 1343 (https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.203016/gov.uscourts.mnd.203016.435.0.pdf).

## 2. "Money to Somalia / al-Shabaab": what the record shows

### Documented in charging documents or DOJ releases (Proven that the allegation was made in a court filing; proven as fact only where a conviction covers it)
1. **Kenya transfers (FOF, "Empire" group).** The USAO release on Sept 4, 2025 says Abdiaziz Farah (convicted, sentenced to 28 years) "sent millions of dollars in fraud proceeds abroad." Among other things, his brother in Kenya said he had received "$1,287,000" in the days before Dec 27, 2021. The release describes a $300,000 transfer labeled "family support," an apartment building in Nairobi, and land in Mandera, Kenya. The brother, Ahmednaji Sheikh, was charged with conspiracy to commit international money laundering (allegation): https://www.irs.gov/compliance/criminal-investigation/kenyan-national-charged-with-international-money-laundering-in-feeding-our-future-fraud-scheme
2. **EIDBI.** Abdinajib Yussuf allegedly "sent more than $200,000 in fraud proceeds to Kenya" (USAO, Dec 18, 2025, IRS-CI mirror above).
3. **HSS.** Asad Adow allegedly used proceeds "to invest in real estate in Kenya" (USAO, Sept 18, 2025). Pristine Health defendants allegedly spent proceeds on travel to London, Sydney, Dubai, Istanbul, and Saudi Arabia (USAO, Dec 18, 2025).
4. **Treasury, official statements.** On Jan 9, 2026, FinCEN issued four notices of investigation to Minnesota money services businesses, a Geographic Targeting Order (reports on transfers of $3,000 or more to beneficiaries outside the U.S. from banks and transmitters in Hennepin and Ramsey counties), and an alert on child-nutrition fraud. Treasury said law enforcement "has limited insight into the persons located abroad who receive the proceeds": https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0354 ; GTO: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/13/2026-00449/geographic-targeting-order-imposing-recordkeeping-and-reporting-requirements-on-certain-financial . The GTO was renewed Aug 7, 2026, with Treasury saying "a portion of which has been laundered overseas." Neither release names Somalia or al-Shabaab: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0597 ; https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/11/2026-16365/geographic-targeting-order-imposing-recordkeeping-and-reporting-requirements-on-certain-financial
5. **Prosecutor on terrorism, Dec 18, 2025.** Thompson said there was "no indication that defendants were sending money to terrorist organizations or supporting terrorist organizations in that way" (reported by MPR from the press conference; no transcript posted).
6. **No terrorism charges.** No defendant in these fraud cases has been charged under **18 U.S.C. 2339B** (material support to a designated FTO) or **18 U.S.C. 2339C** (terrorism financing), based on every DOJ release reviewed.
7. **Earlier state record.** Minnesota's Legislative Auditor (March 2019) was "unable to substantiate the allegation that individuals in Minnesota sent CCAP fraud money to a foreign country where a terrorist organization obtained and used it": https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2019/other/190425.pdf

### Alleged in reporting or by officials (leads; not established by primary record)
- **City Journal (Manhattan Institute), Nov 19, 2025.** Ryan Thorpe and Christopher Rufo's article "Minnesota Welfare Fraud: Some Funds Went to Al-Shabaab" relies largely on unnamed law-enforcement sources and a former investigator: https://www.city-journal.org/article/minnesota-welfare-fraud-somalia-al-shabaab. **Reported, not confirmed by primary record.**
- **Treasury Secretary Bessent (X, Dec 1, 2025, as reported by CBS News).** Treasury is "investigating allegations that ... Minnesotans' tax dollars may have been diverted to the terrorist organization Al-Shabaab." This announces an investigation, not a finding: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/treasury-investigating-minnesota-somalia-al-shabaab-bessent-says/ (CBS blocks automated fetch; confirmed via WebFetch).
- Reporting on trial evidence describes wires to China and Kenya and Kenyan and Turkish real estate. The one Mogadishu reference is a single text about $1,000 (CBS News, Star Tribune). **Reported.** Our keyword search of the government's Bock trial brief found no Kenya, Somalia, or Turkey references.

### Verdicts
| Claim | Label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Minnesota fraud proceeds were sent abroad | **Proven** | Kenyan transfers and property are described in charging documents tied to convicted defendants (Farah). Treasury officially states that a portion was laundered overseas. |
| Proceeds were sent to **Somalia** specifically | **Unresolved (no official finding)** | The documented destinations in the releases we reviewed are Kenya and others. We found no court filing that documents transfers into Somalia. |
| Proceeds **funded al-Shabaab** | **Unresolved (no official finding)** | No charge, no court finding, and no Treasury finding. The Treasury investigation was announced in Dec 2025, but no outcome has been published. The lead prosecutor said in Dec 2025 there was "no indication" of this. The claim rests on City Journal's anonymous sourcing. |
| "Billions" stolen in Minnesota | **Unresolved (no official finding)** | About $375M is charged in federal cases. The $9B figure is a prosecutor's estimate. |

**Neutral note.** Most FOF, HSS, and EIDBI defendants are from Minnesota's Somali community, and the community was also victimized by these schemes (CBS News reporting). Describe individual defendants, not an ethnic group, and never attribute a crime to people who have not been charged.
