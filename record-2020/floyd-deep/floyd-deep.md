## Part A. Plain-English findings

1. **"Homicide" on the death certificate is a medical classification, not a finding of guilt.**
   - The Hennepin County Medical Examiner (Dr. Andrew Baker) ruled the manner of death **Homicide**. He gave the cause as "cardiopulmonary arrest complicating law enforcement subdual, restraint, and neck compression."
   - He also listed as "other significant conditions": "arteriosclerotic and hypertensive heart disease; fentanyl intoxication; recent methamphetamine use."
   - The National Association of Medical Examiners (NAME) manner-of-death guide says homicide means a death that results from another person's volitional act. It says the term "neither indicates nor implies criminal intent." It also says deaths from law-enforcement restraint "may be classified as Homicide," even when the person was already vulnerable because of disease.
   - So the ME's "homicide" ruling did not decide whether Chauvin committed a crime. The jury and the courts decided that.

2. **Under Minnesota law, Floyd's heart disease and drug use did not legally excuse the death.**
   - Minnesota has long required only that the defendant's conduct be a **"substantial causal factor."** It does not have to be the sole cause. A victim's pre-existing condition does not break the chain (State v. Smith, 1962; State v. King, 1985; State v. Gatson, 2011).
   - The jury was instructed in exactly those terms. Judge Cahill had already written in October 2020 that "Floyd's pre-existing medical conditions cannot defeat causation."

3. **The record contains real evidence behind the doubt, and the courts dealt with it on the record.** The main points on the doubting side:
   - Dr. Baker's autopsy found **no physical signs of asphyxia or strangulation**: no petechiae and no neck-structure injury. This is in the charging complaint and in a May 26, 2020 prosecutor memo.
   - A June 1, 2020 prosecutor memo summarizes Baker as calling the fentanyl level (11 ng/mL) "pretty high" and a "fatal level … under normal circumstances."
   - At trial, Baker admitted that if Floyd had been found dead at home alone with that fentanyl level and no trauma, he would have certified a fentanyl death.
   - The defense pathologist, Dr. David Fowler (former Maryland chief ME), testified to a cardiac arrhythmia from heart disease "during restraint." He listed carbon monoxide, drugs and a tumor as contributors.

   On the other side:
   - Three prosecution experts testified that the mechanism was low oxygen from prone restraint: pulmonologist Dr. Martin Tobin, forensic pathologist Dr. Lindsey Thomas, and cardiologist Dr. Jonathan Rich. The toxicologist testified that the methamphetamine level was in the bottom 5.9% of 3,271 DUI cases.
   - The Armed Forces Medical Examiner's office reviewed the case for the U.S. Department of Justice and concurred on cause and manner (homicide).
   - The jury convicted. Judge Cahill later heard the same experts himself in the Thao bench trial and wrote that Fowler's opinion was "unpersuasive." The Court of Appeals affirmed, and the Minnesota Supreme Court and U.S. Supreme Court declined review.
   - Most significant: **in his federal plea agreement, Chauvin himself admitted "that his willful use of unreasonable force resulted in Mr. Floyd's bodily injury and death because his actions impaired Mr. Floyd's ability to obtain and maintain sufficient oxygen."**
   - One nuance the record shows plainly: the prosecution's own experts did not describe the mechanism the same way. Baker described heart disease plus the stress of subdual, restraint and neck compression. Tobin, Thomas and Rich described asphyxia (low oxygen). All of them attributed the death to the restraint.

4. **The one documented ruling against a prosecutor's conduct concerns the Hennepin County Attorney's Office.**
   - In 2020 Judge Cahill barred County Attorney Mike Freeman and three assistants from appearing as trial advocates. The reason was that they had met with Dr. Baker on May 27, 2020 without a non-attorney witness, which created an advocate-witness problem under Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 3.7.
   - The court called that conduct "careless." It found nothing about bias or dishonesty, and let the four keep working on the case behind the scenes.
   - No motion to recuse Judge Cahill or Judge Magnuson appears in the documents reviewed. No court found bias by any judge, prosecutor or the ME.

5. **Outside pressure was documented, and the courts ruled on each instance.**
   - **Rep. Maxine Waters (April 17, 2021)** said "we're looking for a guilty verdict" and that protesters should "get more confrontational." Judge Cahill denied a mistrial, called officials' comments "abhorrent," and said Waters "may have given you something on appeal." On appeal, the conviction was affirmed.
   - **President Biden (April 20, 2021)**, during deliberations, said the evidence was "overwhelming, in my view," noting that he said so only because the jury was sequestered.
   - **The $27 million Minneapolis settlement (announced March 12, 2021, during jury selection)**: the court recalled the seven jurors already seated and excused two of them.
   - **Juror 52** (identified by news as Brandon Mitchell, who spoke publicly **[NEWS LEAD for the name]**): the Court of Appeals found the claim that he hid his attendance at the August 2020 Washington march "contradicted by the record." The questionnaire asked only about demonstrations in Minneapolis.
   - The Court of Appeals acknowledged that pre-trial statements by the Minneapolis police chief and public-safety commissioner calling it "murder" "presupposed guilt." It held that jury screening removed any prejudice.
   - Every venue, continuance, sequestration and new-trial request was denied, and those denials were affirmed.

6. **The people behind the case have ordinary political and professional ties. None rises to a documented conflict.**
   - Judge Peter Cahill was appointed in 2007 by **Republican** Governor Tim Pawlenty. He was a long-time Hennepin County prosecutor, including chief deputy from 2000 to 2006.
   - Prosecutors Jerry Blackwell, Steven Schleicher and Lola Velázquez-Aguilu, and AG Keith Ellison (DFL), gave money to DFL/Democratic candidates. That includes Blackwell's contributions to Ellison's own AG campaign in 2018–2019 and to Gov. Walz. Blackwell was later appointed a federal judge by President Biden (confirmed 51–43, December 2022).
   - Neal Katyal gave to Democrats, including Biden 2020.
   - Federal Judge Paul Magnuson was appointed by **Republican** President Reagan (1981). No FEC or Minnesota contributions were found for him, for defense lawyer Eric Nelson, or for AAG Matthew Frank.
   - None of these ties was the subject of a recusal motion or a court finding in the record reviewed.

7. **Comparable restraint deaths show no consistent pattern of homicide ruling leading to conviction.**
   - Eric Garner, Tony Timpa, Daniel Prude and Manuel Ellis were all ruled homicides by medical examiners even though drugs or heart or lung disease were present. None produced a criminal conviction: no indictment, charges dropped, or acquittal.
   - Elijah McClain's death was ruled "undetermined," yet one officer and two paramedics were convicted.
   - Angelo Quinto's death was ruled an "accident" by a coroner's jury, and no charges were filed.
   - The Floyd case is the outlier in securing a murder conviction. It also had unusually long video-recorded restraint (about 9.5 minutes), expert testimony on the mechanism, and the defendant's own later admission.

8. **Minneapolis officials' 2020 actions are documented.**
   - On June 12, 2020 the City Council unanimously (12–0) adopted Resolution 2020R-152 to create "a transformative new model for cultivating safety." The resolution's text refers to "the murder of George Floyd … by Minneapolis police officers," before any trial.
   - The Council voted 12–0 to advance a charter amendment removing the Police Department from the charter. The Charter Commission delayed it (10–5, Aug. 5, 2020) and then recommended rejection (14–0, Nov. 4, 2020).
   - In November 2021 voters rejected a similar measure, Question 2: **80,506 No (56.17%) to 62,813 Yes (43.83%)**.
   - Kamala Harris's June 1, 2020 post urged donations to the Minnesota Freedom Fund "to help post bail for those protesting on the ground in Minnesota."
   - The June 7, 2020 Powderhorn Park "pledge" is documented here only through news **[NEWS LEAD]**. Council members' original posts were not retrieved.

## Part B. Detail with citations

### 1. The court file

#### 1.1 Charges

- **Original complaint, May 29, 2020** (State v. Chauvin, 27-CR-20-12646).
  - Count I: third-degree murder, Minn. Stat. § 609.195(a). Count II: second-degree manslaughter, § 609.205(1).
  - Approved by prosecutor Amy Sweasy (HCAO). Complainant: BCA SA Michelle Frascone. Signed by Judge Luis Bartolomei. Bail $500,000.
  - It quoted the ME's preliminary findings: "The autopsy revealed no physical findings that support a diagnosis of traumatic asphyxia or strangulation. Mr. Floyd had underlying health conditions including coronary artery disease and hypertensive heart disease. The combined effects of Mr. Floyd being restrained by the police, his underlying health conditions and any potential intoxicants in his system likely contributed to his death."
  - It said the knee was on Floyd's neck "for 8 minutes and 46 seconds." Later court findings put the full restraint at about 9 minutes 29 seconds (COA) and "more than nine minutes and twenty seconds" (Thao verdict).
  - Source: complaint05292020.pdf.
- **Amended complaint, June 3, 2020.**
  - Added Count I: second-degree unintentional murder while committing a felony (§ 609.19 subd. 2(1)), with third-degree assault as the predicate felony. Kept third-degree murder and manslaughter.
  - Approved by AAG Matthew Frank. Bail $1,000,000.
  - Source: amendedcomplaint06032020.pdf. The AG's June 3, 2020 release announced it and aiding-and-abetting charges against Kueng, Lane and Thao, filed jointly with Freeman.
- **Third-degree murder dismissed.** Order on probable cause, Oct. 21, 2020 (order10212020.pdf): third-degree count "GRANTED and that charge is dismissed." Other motions to dismiss were denied.
- **Third-degree murder reinstated.**
  - The court denied the State's first motion to reinstate on Feb. 11, 2021 (order02112021.pdf; motion at motion02042021.pdf).
  - After the Court of Appeals' March 5, 2021 order in A21-0354, applying State v. Noor, and the Supreme Court's denial of review, the count was reinstated on March 11, 2021.
  - *The reinstatement order itself was not retrieved; see Open items.*
  - The jury convicted on that count, but it was never adjudicated (sentencing order). The COA therefore did not reach Chauvin's challenge to it. The Minnesota Supreme Court later narrowed third-degree murder in State v. Noor, 964 N.W.2d 424 (Minn. 2021).
- **Statutes as in force in 2020** (revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2020/cite/…):
  - **§ 609.19 subd. 2(1)**: "causes the death of a human being, without intent to effect the death of any person, while committing or attempting to commit a felony offense other than criminal sexual conduct in the first or second degree with force or violence or a drive-by shooting." Maximum 40 years.
  - **§ 609.195(a)**: "Whoever, without intent to effect the death of any person, causes the death of another by perpetrating an act eminently dangerous to others and evincing a depraved mind, without regard for human life." Maximum 25 years.
  - **§ 609.205(1)**: death caused "by the person's culpable negligence whereby the person creates an unreasonable risk, and consciously takes chances of causing death or great bodily harm to another." Maximum 10 years.
  - **§ 609.223 subd. 1** (the predicate): "Whoever assaults another and inflicts substantial bodily harm." Maximum 5 years, so it is a felony.
  - § 609.02 subd. 7a defines "substantial bodily harm" as including "a temporary but substantial loss or impairment of the function of any bodily member or organ."
  - For comparison: § 609.221 (first degree, great bodily harm) and § 609.222 (second degree, dangerous weapon).

#### 1.3 Jury instructions (filed April 19, 2021): causation

> "'To cause death' … means that the Defendant's act or acts were a substantial causal factor in causing the death of George Floyd. The Defendant is criminally liable for all the consequences of his actions that occur in the ordinary and natural course of events, including those consequences brought about by one or more intervening causes, if such intervening causes were the natural result of the Defendant's acts. The fact that other causes contribute to the death does not relieve the Defendant of criminal liability. However, the Defendant is not criminally liable if a 'superseding cause' caused the death. A 'superseding cause' is a cause that comes after the Defendant's acts, alters the natural sequence of events, and is the sole cause of a result that would not otherwise have occurred."

The jury was also instructed on "Authorized Use of Force by a Police Officer," the defense theory (juryinstructions04192021.pdf).

#### 1.4 Verdict, aggravating factors and sentence

- **Verdicts, April 20, 2021 (forms filed April 21):** guilty on all three counts (verdict1/2/3 04212021.pdf).
- **Blakely findings, May 11, 2021 (order05112021.pdf).**
  - Proven beyond a reasonable doubt: abuse of a position of trust and authority; particular cruelty ("The slow death of George Floyd occurring over approximately six minutes of his positional asphyxia was particularly cruel"); children present; committed as a group with three others.

### 2. What "manner of death: homicide" means

- **NAME, *A Guide for Manner of Death Classification* (1st ed., Feb. 2002; Hanzlick, Hunsaker, Davis):**
  - "Homicide occurs when death results from a volitional act committed by another person to cause fear, harm, or death. Intent to cause death is a common element but is not required … the classification of Homicide for the purposes of death certification is a 'neutral' term and neither indicates nor implies criminal intent, which remains a determination within the province of legal processes."
  - "the manner of death is unnatural when injury hastened the death of one already vulnerable to significant or even life-threatening disease."
  - "Deaths due to positional restraint induced by law enforcement personnel or to choke holds or other measures to subdue may be classified as Homicide. In such cases, there may not be intent to kill, but the death results from one or more intentional, volitional, potentially harmful acts directed at the decedent."
  - The same passage adds that the classification has "some value … toward reducing the public perception that a 'cover up' is being perpetrated."
  - "Seldom, for the purpose of manner-of-death classification, is 'beyond a reasonable doubt' required." Death-certificate findings "only represent the certifier's opinion and are not written in stone or legally binding."
- **Hennepin County ME press release (June 1, 2020):** "Manner of death is not a legal determination of culpability or intent."
- **New York AG Prude report (2021), same principle:** the ME's homicide designation "is not a legal culpability determination"; it means the death occurred "at the hands of another."
- **CDC/NCHS *Medical Examiners' and Coroners' Handbook on Death Registration* (2003):** the file could not be retrieved (CDC blocked automated access). It is not quoted here.
- **Minnesota causation law** (quoted in the Oct. 21, 2020 probable-cause order):
  - A defendant's act is a substantial causal factor if it "contributed to the death" (State v. Torkelson, 404 N.W.2d 352, 357 (Minn. App. 1987)).
  - The State need not prove "the specific mechanism of death," and the act need not be "the sole cause of death" (State v. Gatson, 801 N.W.2d 134, 148 (Minn. 2011)).
  - State v. Smith, 119 N.W.2d 838, 848 (Minn. 1962): "It is immaterial that defendant did not know that the deceased was suffering from a condition which facilitated the killing … Responsibility attaches for an injury which causes or contributes to death although the condition from which the victim was suffering might itself have caused death in time."
  - State v. King, 367 N.W.2d 599, 602 (Minn. App. 1985): the victim's physical condition "will not excuse or minimize its consequences."
  - State v. James, 144 N.W. 216 (Minn. 1913): pneumonia after a stabbing.
  - A superseding cause must be "the sole cause" (Gatson; State v. Jaworsky, 505 N.W.2d 638).
  - The court concluded: "In short, Floyd's pre-existing medical conditions cannot defeat causation."

- **Pattern, stated honestly:**
  1. Medical examiners commonly classify restraint deaths as homicide even when drugs or heart disease are present (Floyd, Garner, Timpa, Prude, Ellis). This is consistent with the NAME guidance.
  2. A homicide classification did not predict criminal conviction. Four of those five produced no criminal conviction.
  3. A non-homicide classification did not prevent conviction (McClain).
  4. "Accident" rulings exist (Quinto) and came from a coroner's inquest jury, not a board-certified ME's certificate.
  5. The Floyd case stands out for the length of the video-recorded restraint, the clinical testimony on low oxygen, and the defendant's own federal admission of causation.
- This is a small, non-random set of well-known cases, not a statistical sample.
