# 2020: The Record

*Sourcing rule for this section: each sentence links to a primary record. These include government documents, court filings, official autopsy and medical-examiner records, commissioned after-action reviews, legislative records, and a person's or organization's own words or filings. Where a source is research data, industry data, a party's legal brief, or a partisan legislative report, the text says so. Journalism was used only to find leads and is not cited as proof. Items that could not be confirmed from a primary source are listed separately in `open-questions.md` and are left out of this section.*

## 1. The death of George Floyd: the complete record

### 1.1 The Hennepin County Medical Examiner's findings

- On June 1, 2020, the Hennepin County Medical Examiner's Office gave the cause of death as "Cardiopulmonary arrest complicating law enforcement subdual, restraint, and neck compression." ([HCME press release, June 1, 2020](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))
- The same release listed the manner of death as "Homicide." ([HCME press release](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))
- Under "How injury occurred" it stated: "Decedent experienced a cardiopulmonary arrest while being restrained by law enforcement officer(s)." ([HCME press release](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))
- Under "Other significant conditions" it listed: "Arteriosclerotic and hypertensive heart disease; fentanyl intoxication; recent methamphetamine use." ([HCME press release](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))
- The release also stated: "Manner of death is not a legal determination of culpability or intent, and should not be used to usurp the judicial process." ([HCME press release](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))
- The full autopsy report was signed by Dr. Andrew M. Baker on June 1, 2020. It records the death as occurring on May 25, 2020 at 9:25 p.m. ([Autopsy report 2020-3700, archived county copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))
- The autopsy lists "Arteriosclerotic heart disease, multifocal, severe," along with hypertensive heart disease and an enlarged heart weighing 540 grams. ([Autopsy report](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))
- It records "No life-threatening injuries identified" and notes no petechiae and no injury to the neck structures or larynx. ([Autopsy report](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))
- The autopsy found sickle cell trait (hemoglobin S 38%), with no sickling before death. It also recorded a positive test for SARS-CoV-2 RNA, which the report judged "most likely" to be leftover positivity from Floyd's known infection in April 2020. ([Autopsy report](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))
- Toxicology on hospital blood drawn before death (NMS Labs) reported the following ([Autopsy report, toxicology section](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf)):
  - fentanyl 11 ng/mL
  - norfentanyl 5.6 ng/mL
  - 4-ANPP 0.65 ng/mL
  - methamphetamine 19 ng/mL
  - delta-9 THC 2.9 ng/mL
  - 11-hydroxy delta-9 THC 1.2 ng/mL
  - delta-9 carboxy THC 42 ng/mL
  - urine morphine (free) 86 ng/mL
- The lab's reference notes in the report state: "In fatalities from fentanyl, blood concentrations are variable and have been reported as low as 3 ng/mL." ([Autopsy report](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))
- The same notes state: "Blood levels of 200 - 600 ng/mL have been reported in methamphetamine abusers who exhibited violent and irrational behavior." ([Autopsy report](http://web.archive.org/web/20200722232711/https://www.hennepin.us/-/media/hennepinus/residents/public-safety/documents/Autopsy_2020-3700_Floyd.pdf))

### 1.2 What the medical examiner told prosecutors before the final report (court exhibits)

- A Hennepin County Attorney's Office memo dated May 26, 2020, filed as an exhibit in *State v. Thao*, records that "[t]he autopsy revealed no physical evidence suggesting that Mr. Floyd died of asphyxiation." It also notes that the final report was not yet complete. ([Exhibit 3, State v. Thao, 27-CR-20-12949](https://mncourts.gov/_media/additionalassets/getattachment/media/stateofminnesotavtouthao/container-documents/content-documents/exhibit-3.pdf?lang=en-US))
- A June 1, 2020 prosecutor's memo in the same filing summarizes a May 31, 2020 call with Dr. Baker about the final toxicology results. It records that Dr. Baker called the fentanyl level of 11 "pretty high," and that he described methamphetamine at 19 ng/mL as "very near the low end" and "a stimulant hard on the heart." ([Exhibit 4, State v. Thao](https://mncourts.gov/_media/additionalassets/getattachment/media/stateofminnesotavtouthao/container-documents/content-documents/exhibit-4.pdf?lang=en-US))
- The memo's summary, written in the memo author's own words, continues: "This level of fentanyl can cause pulmonary edema. Mr. Floyd's lungs were 2-3x their normal weight at autopsy. That is a fatal level of fentanyl under normal circumstances." ([Exhibit 4, State v. Thao](https://mncourts.gov/_media/additionalassets/getattachment/media/stateofminnesotavtouthao/container-documents/content-documents/exhibit-4.pdf?lang=en-US))
- The memo also records, in paraphrase: "AB said that if Mr. Floyd had been found dead in his home (or anywhere else) and there were no other contributing factors he would conclude that it was an overdose death." ([Exhibit 4, State v. Thao](https://mncourts.gov/_media/additionalassets/getattachment/media/stateofminnesotavtouthao/container-documents/content-documents/exhibit-4.pdf?lang=en-US))
- The medical examiner's final certification, issued the same day, listed fentanyl as an "other significant condition" and not as the cause of death. The certified cause and manner were those stated above. ([HCME press release](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MNHENNE/2020/06/01/file_attachments/1464238/2020-3700%20Floyd,%20George%20Perry%20Update%206.1.2020.pdf))

### 1.3 Armed Forces Medical Examiner review

- At the request of the U.S. Department of Justice, the Office of the Armed Forces Medical Examiner reviewed the case (DoD Case Consult C0022-20, dated June 10, 2020). Its report was later filed as Exhibit 5 in *State v. Thao*. ([AFME consult, Exhibit 5](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/exhibit508252020.pdf))
- The AFME wrote: "The Office of the Armed Forces Medical Examiner agrees with the autopsy findings and the cause of death certification of George Floyd as determined by the Hennepin County Medical Examiner's Office." ([AFME consult](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/exhibit508252020.pdf))
- It continued: "His death was caused by the police subdual and restraint in the setting of severe hypertensive atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and methamphetamine and fentanyl intoxication. The subdual and restraint had elements of positional and mechanical asphyxiation. The presence of sickle cell trait is a significant finding in this context." ([AFME consult](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/exhibit508252020.pdf))
- It concluded: "We concur with the reported manner of death of homicide." ([AFME consult](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/exhibit508252020.pdf))

### 1.4 Expert testimony at trial (both sides)

*The trial-testimony summaries below come from Judge Peter Cahill's written Verdict, Findings of Fact, and Conclusions of Law in State v. Thao (filed May 1, 2023). That document cites the Chauvin trial transcript by page.* ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))

**Hennepin County Medical Examiner Dr. Andrew Baker (called by the State)**

- Dr. Baker testified that "the law enforcement subdual restraint and the neck compression was just more than Mr. Floyd could take by virtue of those heart conditions" (Chauvin Tr. 4889–4890). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- He testified that fentanyl and methamphetamine contributed to the death but were not "the direct cause" (Chauvin Tr. 4890–4911). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- He agreed that "[h]ad Mr. Floyd been home alone in his locked residence with no evidence of trauma, and the only autopsy finding was that fentanyl level" he "would certify his death as due to fentanyl toxicity" (Chauvin Tr. 4932). ([Thao verdict, n.60](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- He testified that carbon monoxide "played no role in [Floyd's] death" (Fed. Tr. 1426–1427). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- The court wrote: "The Court finds Dr. Baker's testimony credible." ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))

**Defense expert Dr. David Fowler (forensic pathologist; formerly Chief Medical Examiner for the State of Maryland, per the court's findings)**

- Dr. Fowler testified that the death was caused by "[c]ardiac arrhythmia due to hypertensive atherosclerotic disease during restraint" (Chauvin Tr. 5505). He also testified that Floyd did not die of asphyxia (Chauvin Tr. 5522). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- He testified that several other factors contributed (Chauvin Tr. 5475): possible carbon monoxide from the squad-car exhaust, methamphetamine, fentanyl, and a paraganglioma (a tumor). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- He testified that Chauvin's knee was "nowhere close to [Floyd's] airway" (Chauvin Tr. 5542). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- On cross-examination he made four concessions ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf)):
  - He had not seen any laboratory results for Floyd's carbon monoxide levels (Chauvin Tr. 5565–5568).
  - There was "only a very low level of methamphetamine" (Chauvin Tr. 5497, 5617).
  - Floyd did not show the "outward symptoms" of a fentanyl overdose (Chauvin Tr. 5614).
  - Floyd "should have been given immediate emergency attention" when he went into cardiac arrest (Chauvin Tr. 5604).
- The court wrote: "The Court finds Dr. Fowler's ultimate conclusions and opinions unpersuasive." It noted that he "lacks expertise and clinical experience in pulmonology, cardiology, and toxicology." ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))

**Other prosecution experts**

- Forensic pathologist Dr. Lindsey Thomas testified that asphyxia was the primary mechanism of death and agreed with the homicide certification. She also testified that Floyd would not have died on May 25, 2020 "except for the interactions with law enforcement" (Chauvin Tr. 4753, 4778–4779, 4793–4794). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- NMS Labs toxicologist Dr. Daniel Isenschmid testified to three points (Chauvin Tr. 4610–4633). The court found his testimony credible. ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
  - The methamphetamine level of 19 ng/mL was "very low."
  - That level was in the "bottom 5.9 percent" of 3,271 DUI cases.
  - The median in those DUI cases was 240 ng/mL.
- In the federal trial, Dr. Vikhyat Bebarta, described by the court as "a board-certified emergency physician and medical toxicologist," testified to three points (Fed. Tr. 2128). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
  - Floyd's 11 ng/mL fentanyl level "was also low."
  - Doctors typically give 11 to 20 ng/mL to patients during complex procedures.
  - Patients who die from fentanyl overdoses "typically have levels of 40 ng/mL or higher."
- Pulmonologist Dr. Martin Tobin testified that Floyd's oxygen level had fallen to zero by 8:25:41 p.m. The court found this testimony credible (Tobin, Tr. 4531–4532). ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))

**How the parties characterized the evidence on appeal**

- Chauvin's appellate brief, which is a party's argument and not a finding, stated that Dr. Baker testified to no physical evidence of asphyxia. It also described the fentanyl level as "three times the amount considered fatal." ([Appellant's brief, A21-1228](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12646/brief-appellant.pdf))
- The State's brief, also a party's argument, described Dr. Fowler's opinion as arrhythmia with "significant contributory conditions" (T. 5475). ([Respondent's brief, A21-1228](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12646/brief-respondent.pdf))

### 1.5 The May 6, 2019 arrest

- In the Chauvin trial, the defense called MPD Officer Scott Creighton and paramedic Michelle Moseng to testify about a prior encounter with Floyd on May 6, 2019. ([Thao verdict, n.32](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- Judge Cahill found both witnesses credible. He wrote that "although Floyd displayed some initial indications of noncompliance in the May 6, 2019 incident … none of the officers involved during that incident found it necessary to engage in use of force." ([Thao verdict, n.32](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- Chauvin's appellate brief argued that the trial court limited evidence from the 2019 incident. ([Appellant's brief](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12646/brief-appellant.pdf))

### 1.6 Derek Chauvin: state conviction and appeals

- Testimony in *State v. Chauvin* ran from March 29 to April 15, 2021. The jury returned guilty verdicts on all three charges on April 20, 2021. ([Thao verdict, procedural history](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- The three charges were second-degree unintentional murder, third-degree murder, and second-degree manslaughter. ([Minn. Ct. App. opinion A21-1228](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12646/opinion-published.pdf))
- On June 25, 2021, the court entered a conviction for second-degree unintentional murder and imposed 270 months. ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- The Minnesota Court of Appeals affirmed on April 17, 2023. ([Minn. Ct. App. opinion](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12646/opinion-published.pdf))
- The Minnesota Supreme Court denied review on July 18, 2023. ([Order reproduced in cert. petition appendix, No. 23-416](http://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/23/23-416/285187/20231016171544823_Appendix.pdf))
- The U.S. Supreme Court denied Chauvin's petition for certiorari on November 20, 2023. ([U.S. Supreme Court docket No. 23-416](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/23-416.html))

### 1.7 Derek Chauvin: federal civil rights case

- A federal grand jury indicted Chauvin, Thao, Kueng and Lane on May 6, 2021. ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))
- In his federal plea agreement, Chauvin admitted that he "willfully deprived George Perry Floyd, Jr., of his constitutional rights" and that "[t]his offense resulted in bodily injury to, and the death of George Floyd." ([Plea agreement, D. Minn. 0:21-cr-00108, Doc. 142](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493.142.0_59.pdf))
- He also admitted "that his willful use of unreasonable force resulted in Mr. Floyd's bodily injury and death because his actions impaired Mr. Floyd's ability to obtain and maintain sufficient oxygen to sustain Mr. Floyd's life." ([Plea agreement, Doc. 142](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493.142.0_59.pdf))
- The plea also covered a separate 18 U.S.C. § 242 count for force used on a juvenile on September 4, 2017. ([Amended judgment, Doc. 529](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493.529.0.pdf))
- On July 7, 2022, the court imposed 240 months and 12 months, consecutive (252 months). The judgment adjusted this to 245 months for prior state custody, to run concurrently with the state sentence. ([Amended judgment, Doc. 529](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493/gov.uscourts.mnd.194493.529.0.pdf))

### 1.8 The other three officers

- A federal jury convicted Tou Thao, J. Alexander Kueng and Thomas Lane on all charges after a trial from January 24 to February 21, 2022. ([Thao verdict](https://mncourts.gov/_media/migration/high-profile-cases/27-cr-20-12949-tt/tt-verdict.pdf))

## 2. The 2020 riots: deaths, injuries, damage and prosecutions

### 2.1 How many demonstrations involved violence (research data)

- ACLED, the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project, is a research organization. It reported that "[i]n more than 93% of all demonstrations connected to the movement, demonstrators have not engaged in violence or destructive activity." ([ACLED, "Demonstrations and Political Violence in America: New Data for Summer 2020"](https://acleddata.com/report/demonstrations-and-political-violence-america-new-data-summer-2020))
- ACLED counted more than 7,750 BLM-linked demonstrations in more than 2,440 locations between May 26 and August 22, 2020. ([ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/demonstrations-and-political-violence-america-new-data-summer-2020))
- ACLED reported that violent demonstrations took place in fewer than 220 locations. ([ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/demonstrations-and-political-violence-america-new-data-summer-2020))
- ACLED notes three limits on these figures. ([ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/demonstrations-and-political-violence-america-new-data-summer-2020))
  - Its violent category "includes events where violence may have been initially instigated by police or other actors."
  - The data counts events, not how large or destructive each event was.
  - The period ends August 22, 2020, before the Kenosha unrest.

### 2.2 Property damage

- **Insurance-industry data:** The Insurance Information Institute reports that Property Claim Services (Verisk) designated May 26–June 8, 2020 a multi-state catastrophe. It called this the "costliest civil unrest in U.S. history with insured losses estimated at over $2 billion by PCS (subject to further evaluation)." ([Triple-I, Facts + Statistics: Civil disorders](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-civil-disorders))
- **Insurance-industry data:** For comparison, the same source gives the 1992 Los Angeles riots as $775 million in insured losses, about $1.4 billion in 2020 dollars. ([Triple-I](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-civil-disorders))
- **Minnesota (partisan legislative report):** A joint Minnesota Senate committee report, produced under the Republican majority's committee chairs, stated that "[o]ver 1,500 properties were damaged at a value of over $500 million." ([MN Senate, "Review of Lawlessness and Government Responses to Minnesota's 2020 Riots," Oct. 2020](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf))
- The report cites a newspaper for that damage figure, so the figure itself is not an audited government estimate. ([MN Senate report](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf))

### 2.3 Deaths documented in primary records

- **Minneapolis, Max It Pawn fire:** A federal sentencing memorandum states that Montez Lee poured accelerant and set fire to Max It Pawn, 2726 East Lake Street, on May 28, 2020. ([U.S. sentencing memorandum, U.S. v. Lee, D. Minn. 0:20-cr-00168, Doc. 67](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.189358/gov.uscourts.mnd.189358.67.0_2.pdf))
- The memorandum states that the remains of Oscar Lee Stewart, 30, were found in the rubble on July 20, 2020. The medical examiner attributed his death to "probable inhalation of products of combustion and thermal injury (building fire)." ([U.S. sentencing memorandum, Doc. 67](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.189358/gov.uscourts.mnd.189358.67.0_2.pdf))
- **Minneapolis, other deaths (partisan legislative report):** The Minnesota Senate report lists three deaths "Due to the Riots." ([MN Senate report](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf))
  - Calvin Horton Jr., shot near Cadillac Pawn.
  - The man found in the Max It Pawn fire.
  - A homicide victim found in a car at 17th and Bryant Avenue North.
- **Oakland, federal officer killed:** Federal Protective Service officer Pat Underwood was killed in a drive-by shooting at the Oakland federal courthouse on May 29, 2020. ([U.S. sentencing memorandum, U.S. v. Justus, N.D. Cal. 4:20-cr-00265, Doc. 358](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.361523/gov.uscourts.cand.361523.358.0.pdf))
- Federal prosecutors wrote that the attackers were "two adherents of the extremist 'Boogaloo' movement." They wrote that the attackers "chose to strike in the vicinity of a large protest at Frank Ogawa Plaza over the killing of George Floyd, in order to avoid apprehension and in the hopes of sparking additional unrest." ([U.S. sentencing memorandum, Doc. 358](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.361523/gov.uscourts.cand.361523.358.0.pdf))
- **Seattle, the "CHOP" zone:** See section 5.3 for the two homicides the City of Seattle documented in and around the Capitol Hill protest area.
- No single official national count of deaths linked to the 2020 unrest was found in primary government records. See `open-questions.md`.

### 2.4 Federal prosecutions

- On September 24, 2020, the U.S. Department of Justice announced that "more than 300 individuals in 29 states and Washington, D.C., have been charged for crimes committed adjacent to or under the guise of peaceful demonstrations since the end of May." ([DOJ press release, Sept. 24, 2020, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20220118032009/https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/over-300-people-facing-federal-charges-crimes-committed-during-nationwide-demonstrations))
- The release stated: "Approximately 80 individuals have been charged with offenses relating to arson and explosives." ([DOJ release, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20220118032009/https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/over-300-people-facing-federal-charges-crimes-committed-during-nationwide-demonstrations))
- It also stated that about 35 people had been charged with assaulting law enforcement officers and related offenses. It said that "[o]ne of these cases was charged in Massachusetts; the rest of these individuals were charged in Oregon." ([DOJ release, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20220118032009/https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/over-300-people-facing-federal-charges-crimes-committed-during-nationwide-demonstrations))
- The release listed fire damage to several public buildings, including the "Portland Courthouse, Nashville Courthouse, Minneapolis Police Third Precinct, Seattle Police East Precinct." ## 4. The "peaceful" framing, in officials' and broadcasters' own words

- On CNN on June 11, 2020, Seattle Mayor Jenny Durkan described the Capitol Hill protest zone as "more like a block party atmosphere. It's not an armed takeover." ([CNN transcript, Cuomo Prime Time, June 11, 2020](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/CPT/date/2020-06-11/segment/01))
- In the same interview Durkan said that "there is no threat right now to the public." ([CNN transcript, June 11, 2020](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/CPT/date/2020-06-11/segment/01))

- Durkan's own Executive Order 2020-08, dated June 30, 2020, later documented shootings in the area on June 20, June 22 and June 29. It also documented a 525% increase in person-related crime there compared with the same period in 2019 (see section 5.3). ([Seattle Executive Order 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))
- CNN's *Early Start* broadcast of August 25, 2020 carried the on-screen caption "FIERY BUT MOSTLY PEACEFUL PROTESTS AFTER POLICE SHOOTING" while showing a reporter in Kenosha, Wisconsin, in front of burning structures. ([CNN transcript, Early Start, Aug. 25, 2020](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/es/date/2020-08-25/segment/01))

- Seattle's own executive order on the protest zone stated that "much of the expression has been peaceful." The same order documented the shootings and the crime increase. ([Seattle EO 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))

## 5. The response and the failures to contain the violence

### 5.1 Minneapolis and the State of Minnesota

- The City of Minneapolis commissioned an independent after-action review from Hillard Heintze. The report was issued March 7, 2022 and covers May 25–June 3, 2020. ([Hillard Heintze, "An After-Action Review of City Agencies' Responses to Activities Directly Following George Floyd's Death on May 25, 2020"](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- The review found that "the City of Minneapolis did not use its emergency operations plan effectively to guide its response." ([After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- The review states that on May 28, 2020, "the MPD chief announced the decision to abandon the 3rd Precinct. At 10:15 p.m., MPD leadership ordered the 3rd Precinct evacuated." ([After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- It continues: "Once the MPD abandoned the 3rd Precinct, rioters assumed control of the building and quickly set fires within and to additional buildings in the area." ([After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- The Minnesota Senate report's MPD timeline lists "2155 hours – Order given to Evacuate the 3rd precinct" and "2215 hours – PRECINCT 3 IS COMPROMISED." ([MN Senate report](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf))
- The two documents give different times for the evacuation order: 9:55 p.m. in the Senate report and 10:15 p.m. in the city's review. ([MN Senate report](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf); [After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- **National Guard, the city review's account:** The city review found that the Mayor made a verbal request to the Governor on the evening of May 27, followed by a written request. It found that "the detailed information critical to the mission, required by MNG for approval as detailed in the policy, was not included in any of the initial requests." ([After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- The same review found that "subsequent requests on May 28 included the required information and fulfilled the MNG requirements for activation." ([After-Action Review](https://kstp.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/2020-Civil-Unrest-After-Action-Review-Report.pdf))
- **National Guard, the Senate majority's account:** The Senate report stated that "Governor Walz first mobilized the Minnesota National Guard on the afternoon of Thursday, May 28, 18 hours after Mayor Frey requested assistance." It added that the Guard "was not fully mobilized until four days after the first building was burned." ([MN Senate report](https://assets.senate.mn/committees/2019-2020/3102_Committee_on_Transportation_Finance_and_Policy/Review%20of%20Lawlessness%20and%20Government%20Responses%20to%20Minnesota%27s%202020%20Riots.pdf))
- Governor Walz's Executive Order 20-64, signed May 28, 2020, declared a peacetime emergency and directed that the Adjutant General order the Guard to state active duty. ([EO 20-64](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/EO%2020-64%20Final_tcm1055-433855.pdf))
- EO 20-64 stated that "some individuals have engaged in unlawful and dangerous activity, including arson, rioting, looting." ([EO 20-64](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/EO%2020-64%20Final_tcm1055-433855.pdf))
- It also stated that "the Mayors of both cities have requested the assistance of the Minnesota National Guard." ([EO 20-64](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/EO%2020-64%20Final_tcm1055-433855.pdf))
- Executive Order 20-65 imposed a curfew in Minneapolis and Saint Paul from 8:00 p.m. Friday, May 29 to 6:00 a.m. Saturday, May 30, and again the following night. ([EO 20-65](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/EO%2020-65%20Final_tcm1055-434635.pdf))

### 5.2 Portland

- In 2020, the Oregon Legislature passed HB 4208, which bars law enforcement from using tear gas for crowd control "except in circumstances constituting a riot." ([Enrolled HB 4208, 2020 First Special Session](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2020S1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4208/Enrolled))
- HB 4208 also requires two announcements and time to evacuate before tear gas is used. ([Enrolled HB 4208](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2020S1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB4208/Enrolled))
- On September 10, 2020, Mayor Ted Wheeler, acting as police commissioner, stated: "effective immediately and until further notice, I am directing the Portland Police to end the use of CS gas for crowd control." ([City of Portland, Mayor Wheeler directive, Sept. 10, 2020](https://www.portland.gov/wheeler/news/2020/9/10/mayor-wheeler-directive-use-cs-gas))
- In the same statement Wheeler said: "During the last hundred days Portland, Multnomah County and State Police have all relied on CS gas where there is a threat to life safety." ([City of Portland](https://www.portland.gov/wheeler/news/2020/9/10/mayor-wheeler-directive-use-cs-gas))
- He also said: "Arson, vandalism, and violence are not going to drive change in this community." ([City of Portland](https://www.portland.gov/wheeler/news/2020/9/10/mayor-wheeler-directive-use-cs-gas))
- In an August 5, 2020 readout, the U.S. Department of Homeland Security stated that on the prior night "rioters breached the doors to the [Portland Police Association] building and started a fire inside, according to PPB reports. PPB then declared a riot." ([DHS, Portland Riots Read-out: August 5](https://www.dhs.gov/archive/news/2020/08/05/portland-riots-read-out-august-5))
- **Federal-state dispute:** Oregon's Attorney General sued federal agencies on July 17, 2020, seeking to stop federal officers from seizing protesters in Portland. On July 24, 2020, U.S. District Judge Michael Mosman denied a temporary restraining order, finding "the State of Oregon lacks standing." ([Opinion and Order, Rosenblum v. John Does 1-10, D. Or. 3:20-cv-01161, Doc. 23](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ord.153632/gov.uscourts.ord.153632.23.0_4.pdf))
- Judge Mosman ruled "without reaching the merits of the underlying claims." ([Opinion and Order, Doc. 23](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ord.153632/gov.uscourts.ord.153632.23.0_4.pdf))
- Oregon later dismissed the case without prejudice, "[i]n light of the defendant agencies having evidently ceased the conduct at issue." ([Notice of voluntary dismissal, Doc. 28](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ord.153632/gov.uscourts.ord.153632.28.0_1.pdf))

### 5.3 Seattle and the CHAZ/CHOP zone

- The Seattle Office of Inspector General's city-commissioned Sentinel Event Review reports: "On the evening of June 8th, SPD evacuated its East Precinct." ([Seattle OIG Sentinel Event Review, Wave 3](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/sentinel%20event%20review/wave3reportfinal.pdf))
- The review states: "For more than a year after the evacuation, it was unclear who authorized SPD to vacate the precinct and why." ([Seattle OIG SER Wave 3](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/sentinel%20event%20review/wave3reportfinal.pdf))
- The review states: "The CHOP existed until July 1, 2020." ([Seattle OIG SER Wave 3](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/sentinel%20event%20review/wave3reportfinal.pdf))
- On June 20, 2020, the Seattle Police Department reported that a 19-year-old man was killed in a shooting inside the protest area. It said officers were "met by a violent crowd that prevented officers safe access to the victims." ([SPD Blotter, June 20, 2020](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2020/06/20/homicide-investigation-inside-protest-area/))
- On June 29, 2020, SPD reported that a 16-year-old was killed and a 14-year-old injured in a shooting on 12th Avenue. It said that "the crime scene had been disturbed." ([SPD Blotter, June 29, 2020](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2020/06/29/one-dead-one-injured-in-early-morning-shooting-on-12th-avenue/))
- Mayor Durkan's Executive Order 2020-08 states that after the June 20 shooting, first responders "were denied safe access to the area by hostile crowds, including armed individuals." ([Seattle EO 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))
- The order states that on June 29, "a juvenile was shot and killed," and that "[e]vidence indicates that this murder may have been committed by individual(s) 'occupying' the area." ([Seattle EO 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))
- The order records "[a]n increase of 525%, 22 additional incidents, in person-related crime … between June 2nd and June 30th, 2020, compared to the same period of time in 2019." That increase included two additional homicides, six additional robberies and sixteen additional aggravated assaults. ([Seattle EO 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))
- The order states: "A pervasive presence of firearms and other weapons has been well-documented." ([Seattle EO 2020-08](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2020/07/Executive-Order-2020-08_Directive-City-Depts_Cal-Anderson-Park-Area.pdf))
- SPD announced on July 1, 2020 that the Mayor had issued an emergency order regarding the protest zone. ([SPD Blotter, July 1, 2020](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2020/07/01/mayor-durkan-issues-emergency-order-regarding-capitol-hill-protest-zone/))
- The Sentinel Event Review reports that the Office of Police Accountability found that "an SPD Captain appointed an officer to make false radio broadcasts to draw protestors away from the CHOP." The false broadcasts, on June 8, 2020, reported Proud Boys moving toward the zone, and OPA found they violated SPD policy. ([Seattle OIG SER Wave 3](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/sentinel%20event%20review/wave3reportfinal.pdf))
- The review also reports that "No police reports were filed related to extortion of businesses within or around the CHOP." This came after SPD said at a June 10 press conference that it had "heard anecdotally reports" of extortion. ([Seattle OIG SER Wave 3](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/oig/sentinel%20event%20review/wave3reportfinal.pdf))
- The Seattle City Council passed an ordinance banning police use of crowd-control weapons. On July 24, 2020, at the U.S. government's request, U.S. District Judge James Robart granted the government's motion for a temporary restraining order. The order enjoined the ordinance's "implementation date" until the city followed the procedures in its federal consent decree, and the court stated that it "does not enjoin the CCW Ordinance itself." ([TRO, United States v. City of Seattle, W.D. Wash. 2:12-cv-01282](https://www.wawd.uscourts.gov/sites/wawd/files/TRO%20Order%2012-1282.pdf))

### 5.4 Federal response

- A September 2, 2020 presidential memorandum directed a review of federal funding to "anarchist jurisdictions." It stated that "for more than 3 weeks, beginning on June 9, 2020, the city of Seattle allowed anarchists and rioters to take over six square blocks of the city." ([Presidential Memorandum, Sept. 2, 2020](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/memorandum-reviewing-funding-state-local-government-recipients-permitting-anarchy-violence-destruction-american-cities/))
- The memorandum also stated that "[i]n Portland, for over 80 days, State and local officials have allowed violent anarchists to unlawfully riot." This is the administration's characterization. ([Presidential Memorandum](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/memorandum-reviewing-funding-state-local-government-recipients-permitting-anarchy-violence-destruction-american-cities/))
