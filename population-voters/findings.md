# Findings: U.S. population, citizenship and voter registration, 2005–2025 (plus claim checks)

Prepared Sep 24, 2026 (MT). All figures are published values; see the workbook's Sources sheet for exact URLs. The only arithmetic is year-over-year differences, plus one requested Biden-period sum.

## Population and citizenship
- **Citizens.** ACS counts 277.6M U.S. citizens in 2006 and 315.7M in 2024. Noncitizens were 21.8M in 2006, 20.2M in the 2020 experimental estimate (not comparable) and 24.4M in 2024.
- **Comparability.** The 2005 figures cover households only. The 2020 figures are experimental and have no CVAP table. The 2025 ACS has not been released.
- **CVAP** (citizens 18+) rose from 206.3M (2006) to 245.3M (2024).
- **PEP resident population** was 295.5M (2005) and 341.8M (2025).

## Voter registration
- **EAVS registrations** (state administrative counts, including inactive records) were 172.8M (2006), 189.8M (2008), 214.1M (2016), 228.0M (2020), 226.3M (2022) and 234.5M (2024).
- **Documented reasons for changes.** EAC documents one: the 2004→2006 decline, which it attributes to HAVA statewide databases removing duplicates. The 2014 figure is not comparable because some states reported active voters only. For other cycles, the sources pulled give no cause.
- **CPS self-reported registration** of citizens was 135.8M (2006) and 173.9M (2024), in thousands in the source. EAVS and CPS measure different things (see the Discrepancies sheet).
- **Native-born vs naturalized.** There is no administrative count of registrants by native-born vs naturalized status. CPS self-reports show naturalized-citizen registrants of 7.6M (2006) and 15.9M (2024).
- **Removals** are routine and large: 16.7M (2016), 17.3M (2018), 18.8M (2020), 19.3M (2022) and 21.3M (2024).
  - Main reasons: moved; died; failed to respond to an NVRA notice and did not vote in two federal elections.
  - The 2024 errata revised several reason counts.
- **New valid registrations** were 28.9M (2016), 20.9M (2018), 33.4M (2020), 22.8M (2022) and 26.2M (2024).
- **Registration method.** Motor-vehicle offices and AVR supply the largest share. In 2024 there were 31.8M MVA and 26.1M AVR transactions out of 103.5M.

## Naturalization
- **FY2007 and FY2008.** Applications jumped to 1.38M in FY2007, ahead of the July 2007 fee increase and the 2008 election. Persons naturalized peaked at 1,046,540 in FY2008.
- **FY2020.** Naturalizations dipped to 628,250 during COVID-19 office closures.
- **FY2022.** They reached 969,380 during backlog reduction.
- **Source caveat.** The reason text comes from DHS flow reporting and was not re-verified from the box.
- **FY2025** has not been published.

## Births, deaths, SSNs
- **Births** fell from 4.32M (2007 peak) to 3.63M (2024). The provisional 2025 figure is 3.61M.
- **Deaths** peaked at 3.46M (2021). They were 3.07M in 2024 and 3.09M provisional in 2025.
- **SSNs issued** were 5.3M (2005), 4.5M (2020) and 7.5M (2024). The under-15 share fell from 83.9% (2020) to 56.1% (2024).
- **SSN citizenship split.** SSA does not publish a citizen/noncitizen split or Enumeration-at-Birth counts in these tables.

## Noncitizens and benefits (eligible and ineligible kept separate)
- **SSI.** Noncitizen recipients, who must meet PRWORA qualified-alien exceptions, fell from 680,397 (Dec 2005, 9.6% of recipients) to 319,391 (Dec 2024, 4.3%).
- **SNAP, FY2023** (eligible groups):
  - Refugees/asylees: 1.1% of participants.
  - Other eligible noncitizens: 3.3%.
  - U.S.-born citizens: 89.4%.
- **Emergency Medicaid, FY2023.** Spending was about $3.8B, per CBO as summarized by KFF; the CBO PDF was blocked from the box. It covers undocumented people and lawfully present people within the 5-year bar who meet other Medicaid rules.
- **Not reported.** No official estimate isolating improper payments to ineligible noncitizens was found.

## Noncitizens on voter rolls: official counts are small
- **Georgia (2024):** 20 of about 8.2M registrants.
- **Texas (2024):**
  - The Governor said 6,500+ "potential noncitizens" were removed.
  - Records reviewed by the Texas Tribune show 581 identified as noncitizens.
- **Texas (2025–26 SAVE run):** 2,724 flagged; 578 later proven citizens; 117 referred to the AG.
- **Louisiana:** 390 suspected; 79 had voted since the 1980s.
- **Iowa:** 277 (initially announced as more than 2,000); 35 counted ballots in 2024.
- **Ohio:** referrals of 597 (2024) and 30 (Jun 2025).
- **Colorado (2013):** 155 referrals; 4 charged.
- **New Jersey (item J).**
  - A Motor Vehicle Commission (MVC) software error registered about 6,600 people who said they were not citizens (Jun 2023–Jun 2024).
  - About 5,100 were deleted and about 1,450 blocked pending county review. About 340 voted; about 220 more are under review (Governor's releases, Jul 21 and Aug 19, 2026).
- **National:**
  - DHS SAVE flagged 28,635 potential noncitizens while verifying 65M+ voters.
  - DOJ charged 70 people (Jan 2025–Sep 2026).
  - HSI reported 160 arrests.
- **Where the errors come from.** Many registrations of noncitizens came from government processing errors rather than individual attempts.

## Item I: the "30,000 illegal aliens registered" TV report (about Sep 23, 2026)
- **Likely source.** The figure traces to a **Reuters investigation published Sep 23, 2026** (carried by USA Today and others). It found that states "may have added more than 30,000 self-declared noncitizens" to voter rolls **since 2000**, across 12 states, **because of DMV software glitches and clerical errors**.
- **What Reuters does not say:**
  - It does not say they were "illegal aliens"; the group includes lawful permanent residents and other lawful noncitizens.
  - It does not say they voted: "Reuters was not able to determine how many... actually cast a ballot."
- **Primary records Reuters relied on:** New Jersey (about 6,600), Virginia ELECT annual reports, Iowa (277; 35 ballots), California DMV 2018 (up to 1,500), Oregon, Texas, Ohio, South Dakota and others.
- **What I could not find.** I could not identify the specific TV segment.
- **Label.** If a broadcast said "30,000 illegal aliens registered" as a current or single-jurisdiction fact, it is **Rated misleading**. The figure is cumulative over 26 years in 12 states, counts self-declared noncitizens of any status, and says nothing about voting.

## Item H: Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold
- **2022 postcards (facts).**
  - On Sep 27, 2022, the Colorado SOS mailed ERIC-required registration-information postcards (not forms or ballots) to about 30,000 noncitizens; a later count was 31,093.
  - Cause: a Department of Revenue list lacked the formatting needed to screen out noncitizen license holders.
  - The office said the online system rejects noncitizen licenses and SSNs, that it would compare the recipient list to the rolls daily, and that it would send corrective notices.
  - It was not aware of any recipient who registered. No follow-up count of registrations was found.
  - Sources: AP/CBS Colorado Oct 11, 2022; AFP Oct 13, 2022.
- **"Dead and non-citizens should vote" TV statement: Unsupported.** No record of Griswold saying this was found. Her documented statements describe removing deceased voters using state health and SSA death data, and rejecting noncitizen registrations.

## Item K: Judicial Watch voter-roll cases (court records vs claims)
- **LA County** (*JW v. Logan*, C.D. Cal. 2:17-cv-08948, settled Jan 2019).
  - The settlement covers about 1,565,000 inactive registrations and requires NVRA notices, with cancellation of non-responders who miss the required federal elections.
  - Judicial Watch's headline said "remove 1.5 million."
- **Kentucky** (*JW v. Grimes*, E.D. Ky. 3:17-cv-94): consent judgment entered Jul 3, 2018 requiring a list-maintenance program.
- **Colorado** (*JW v. Griswold*, D. Colo. 20-cv-02992).
  - Settled Mar 2023: annual data reporting through 2028, dismissal without prejudice, no admission of liability.
  - The court's Feb 27, 2024 order records that the Secretary's discovery figure of 306,303 8(d)(1)(B) removals was erroneous. Judicial Watch said the correct figure was 101,607.
  - EAVS 2022 shows 161,607 Colorado removals for failure to respond to confirmation notices.
- **North Carolina.** Settled 2022 after more than 430,000 inactive registrations were removed, per Judicial Watch; not verified from state records.
- **Pennsylvania.** Settled 2023 with reporting terms, per secondary summary; not verified from the docket.
- **Related Supreme Court case.** *Husted v. A. Philip Randolph Institute* (2018) upheld Ohio's supplemental process; it was not a Judicial Watch case.

## Item L: Harris "purging voter rolls" (details in harris-rolls-claim.md)
- **Where she said it.** At a Detroit NAACP forum on Tue Sep 22, 2026 (not a TV interview), Harris said: "They are cheating by purging voter rolls... check your voter registration status to make sure you haven't been purged."
- **She did not name Trump** in the transcribed passage.
- **The record:**
  - No federal agency has removed anyone from a state roll; states keep the rolls under the NVRA.
  - The administration has pressed states through EO 14248 and EO 14399, DOJ list suits (mostly dismissed), SAVE bulk checks (vacated Jun 2026; stay pending at SCOTUS) and a USCIS initiative (whistleblower allegations).
  - Some states cancelled registrations using SAVE data. In Texas, 578 of 2,724 flagged were later shown to be citizens and ordered reinstated if removed.
- **Label for "Trump is purging the voter rolls": Rated misleading.** The advice to check registration is not rated.

## Colorado
- **Population and citizenship.**
  - PEP: 4.63M (2005) to 6.01M (2025).
  - ACS noncitizens: 338,446 (2006), 279,174 (2019) and 332,000 (2024).
  - CVAP: 4.45M (2024).
- **Registration.** EAVS registrations were 3.00M (2006) and 4.58M (2024). The 2024 total includes 508,668 inactive.
- **All-mail voting.** Colorado went all-mail under HB13-1303 (2013). Mail ballots counted:
  - 39.6% of ballots in 2006.
  - 94.8% in 2014.
  - 2,957,550 of 3,240,754 participants in 2024 (91.3% by mail per EAVS).
- **Letter check.** All seven claims in Sen. Hickenlooper's Mar 20, 2026 SAVE Act letter are rated **Accurate**, with context (see letter-factcheck.md). The SAVE America Act passed the House Feb 11, 2026 (218–213) and stalled in the Senate in March 2026.

## Humanitarian status (Biden period, Jan 20, 2021–Jan 20, 2025)
- **Sums for FY2021–FY2024** (FY2021 includes about 3.7 months under Trump; FY2025 is not yet published):
  - Refugee arrivals: 197,080.
  - Asylum grants: 171,330 (76,910 affirmative; 94,420 defensive).
- **Annual trend.** Refugee arrivals rose from 11,450 (FY2021) to 100,060 (FY2024). Asylum grants rose from 16,450 to 68,170.
- **Parole:**
  - CHNV parole: about 532,000 (Oct 2022–Jan 2025).
  - CBP One: more than 936,500 appointments scheduled (Jan 2023–Dec 2024).
  - Both ended in 2025. The Supreme Court allowed the CHNV terminations (May 30, 2025).
- **DACA:** 436,350 active recipients (Jun 30, 2026).
- **Not reported as annual series:** TPS, OAW, U4U, border parole, and I-765 approvals by category. USCIS publishes I-765 data quarterly only, and summing quarters is prohibited.
- **No amnesty law** has been enacted since IRCA (1986).
